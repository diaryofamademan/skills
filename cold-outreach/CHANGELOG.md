## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-16

First build. Named cold-outreach: the name states the job in plain words. The skill writes and judges cold email and cold DMs; it never sends.

The nine-step procedure (test the offer, build the batch from a trigger, warm path then history gate, check the sender's surface, research one true observation, form the hypothesis, write the first touch, write the follow-ups, judge and return for approval) was built from the author's own sent outreach across data licensing, advisory, and sponsorship lanes, then audited against two practitioner sources — Lavender's 20 cold email tips (8 adopted, 10 adapted, 2 overridden, each with its reason) and Dimitar Angelov's outbound article (its offer test, funnel diagnostic, and batch-by-segment adopted; its volume model, fast cadence, pattern-interrupt subjects, and first-touch calendar link rejected for small, high-value lanes).

A graded evidence pass over the academic literature and the large platform datasets shaped the rules and their labels: the interest-question ask (Gong, 304,174 emails), trigger-based personalization as the largest measured lever (Gong, 85 million emails), follow-ups carrying the majority of replies and meetings (Belkins, 7.53 million emails), and short plain copy (with the length optimum explicitly labeled contested between Gong and Belkins). Claims the evidence killed are recorded in the references so they are not re-imported: loss framing as a persuasion rule, the 1942 "5–12 touches" statistic, the sourceless "+142% personalization" figure, and the "HTML bounces 674% more" claim.

Three passes from the author's other playbooks are built in: first-principles (the reply chain — person, trigger, offer, then copy — and approval time as the scarce input), fundraising (qualify fast, a fast no is a win, proof sequenced across touches, momentum claims only when true), and game theory (small repeated games tax one-shot tricks; the sender's surface is part of the game; making "no" free raises total replies). Field tests in the author's three lanes are planned before the skill is treated as proven; the benchmarks it will be judged against are the author's own previous batches, not vendor headline rates.

# Sources and credits

Every source behind cold-outreach, with what was taken from each. Academic sources are the anchors; vendor datasets are used with their size, year, and commercial interest stated; practitioner sources were audited, not adopted whole (see `lavender-audit.md`). Paraphrases and adaptations throughout; no source text is reproduced.

## Academic (grade A)

| Source | What was taken |
| --- | --- |
| Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta, "Personalization in Email Marketing: The Role of Noninformative Advertising Content," *Marketing Science* 37(2), 2018 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v37y2018i2p236-258.html | The field's only randomized field evidence on personalization: a first name in a marketing email's subject line, +20% opens, +31% leads — and its limits (opt-in marketing email, not cold B2B) |
| 2023 *Marketing Letters* replication of first-name personalization | The counterweight: first-name personalization slightly negative, title-plus-surname positive — token effects are context-dependent |
| O'Keefe & Jensen, framing meta-analysis, *Communication Yearbook* 2006; updates 2007 and Gallagher & Updegraff, *Annals of Behavioral Medicine* 2012 — https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2006-08561-001 | Loss frames are not generally more persuasive (165 effects, N=50,780); gain frames win prevention behaviors by r = .03–.083. The basis for excluding loss framing |
| Brown, Imai, Vieider & Camerer, *Journal of Economic Literature* 62(2), 2024 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/jeclit/v62y2024i2p485-516.html | The modern loss-aversion estimate: λ = 1.955 across 607 estimates |
| Walasek, Mullett & Stewart, *Journal of Economic Psychology* 2024 — https://wrap-test.warwick.ac.uk/185745/13/1-s2.0-S0167487024000485-main.pdf | λ = 1.31 under stricter inclusion — the live disagreement that refutes quoting 2.25 as settled |
| Yechiam & Zeif, *Journal of Economic Psychology* 2025 | λ ≈ 1.07 and not significant in symmetric designs |

## Large datasets (grade B)

| Source | What was taken |
| --- | --- |
| Gong, "Does cold email even work any more?" (28M+ emails, 2025) — https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says | The ~100-word ceiling; product-first pitching cutting replies up to 57% |
| Gong × 30MPC × Outbound Squad, *The Ultimate Cold Email Data Report* (85M+ emails, 2025) — https://tactics.30mpc.com/hubfs/The%20Ultimate%20Cold%20Email%20Data%20Report-1.pdf | The length brackets (peak 51–100 words); the personalization-type index (trigger 24, company 9, individual 6, baseline 2); offer +28% and meeting ask −44% on replies; body proof +41%, subject proof −4%; short and lowercase subject findings; the empty-subject result |
| Gong, "How to Write a Sales Email That Converts" (304,174-email CTA study; 132k ROI-language study; updated 2026) — https://www.gong.io/blog/how-to-write-a-sales-email | The interest ask beating the time ask 2×+ cold, and the time ask winning in-deal (15% → 37%); ROI language −15% |
| Belkins, *Sales Follow-Up Statistics in B2B* (7,530,489 emails, 2025) — https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics | Steps 2–6 = 58.6% of replies; steps 3–5 = 53.5% of meetings; the 3–5 step sweet spot; 0.45% replies per send and ~1 meeting per 6,275 sends; midweek-morning timing |
| Belkins, cold email length and subject-line studies (16.5M and 5.5M emails) — https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics | The contradiction on length (101–200 words ranked best) and the subject-line data (2–4 words; personalized subjects 7% vs 3% replies; case effectively tied) |
| Belkins × Expandi, LinkedIn outreach study (15.1M contacts, 2025) — https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study | LinkedIn reply rates: 7.2% overall, 12.22% to existing connections, 7.5% Open InMail; the connection-note paradox; 1.3% of connected prospects booking |
| Smartlead, *The State of Cold Email 2026* (850M+ emails) — https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks/state-of-cold-email | Under-50-words ranking; day-of-week near-null (0.82–0.91%); the ~7am recipient-time peak; 0.74% median replies per contact |
| Boomerang, email response study (5.3M general emails, 2016), via published summaries — https://money.com/how-to-get-people-to-respond-to-emails/ | Third-grade reading level +36% response; 50–125 words; 1–3 questions +50% — all labeled general email, never cold |
| HubSpot, plain text vs HTML (>500M marketing emails plus A/B tests) — https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data | Simpler creative winning clicks (plain +42% over a GIF version; HTML+images −51%); equal deliverability for properly coded HTML |
| Salesloft / Drift, prospecting data (15M emails, 2020), and Salesloft cadence benchmarks — https://www.salesloft.com/resources/blog/sales-prospecting | ~15% personalized content replying at 2.75× unpersonalized; video in cadence emails +26% replies (grade B−) |
| Backlinko × Pitchbox (12M outreach emails, 2019) | Personalization +30.5%/+32.7% on responses; one follow-up +65.8% — PR outreach, labeled as such wherever used |
| Hunter, cold email datasets (11M emails 2024; 31M emails 2025) | Small-campaign advantage (≤50 recipients 5.8% vs 1,000+ at 2.1%); sales-outreach reply rate 3.0% (2025) |
| Instantly, *Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026* (billions of interactions, 2025 data) — https://instantly.ai/report-page | 3.43% mean reply rate; the declining trajectory (5.1% → 3.43%) |
| Outreach.io sequence benchmarks — https://support.outreach.io/support/solutions/articles/159000425763-outreach-s-best-practices-for-personalizing-emails | The 23% positive-sentiment standard for cold sequences (a target, used as a range anchor) |

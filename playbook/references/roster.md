# The roster

Every Made Man Playbook skill the router can select, in the order the router runs them. For each skill: what it **owns**, its **triggers** (the task shapes that should select it), what it **consumes**, and what it **hands off**.

The roster is a living list. A newly published playbook skill is added to this roster in the same pass that installs it, and an entry is re-read against its skill's frontmatter whenever that skill's version changes. An entry that no longer matches the skill it describes is a defect, not a summary.

Two standing support skills sit outside this roster and are never selected per task: **work-humanizer** and **writing-styles**. The writing pass in step 7 of the router's procedure calls them on every merged deliverable.

## Understand

### first-principles-thinking

- **Owns:** stripping a decision, design, or cost down to fundamental truths that carry named evidence, pricing the floor where money is involved, and rebuilding a better option from the floor up instead of reasoning by analogy.

- **Triggers:** a cost that has always been high and nobody can explain; a design or process inherited from convention; a strategy decision where every option is a slight variation on what competitors already do; a stuck problem where incremental fixes keep failing; a build-versus-buy call that needs a floor price first.

- **Consumes:** the decision named in one sentence, with its unit and success number; the assumption list, each with its source; prices, measurements, and binding documents as evidence.

- **Hands off:** a rebuilt option compared against the status quo, with floor math, an idiot index where money is involved, a reasoning trace that cites its truths, and a fix list with owners. Hands flow problems to fix-the-bottleneck and actor-dependent unknowns to game-theory-for-decisions.

### fix-the-bottleneck

- **Owns:** the throughput of a system that already runs: measuring every stage of the chain, naming the one constraint the queues point at, and relieving it in the order exploit, subordinate, elevate.

- **Triggers:** a running system whose output is below target; whack-a-mole fixing where local metrics improve and total output does not; a queue nobody can explain; a capacity decision — hire, buy, add machines, add agents; a single point of failure everything routes through.

- **Consumes:** the chain's stage measurements — input rates, success rates, wait times, work in progress — or the admission that they do not exist yet, in which case its deliverable is the instrumentation plan.

- **Hands off:** the goal stated as throughput, the chain map, the one constraint verdict with its kind and its loss classes, and the exploit, subordinate, and elevate lists with a re-measure plan. Hands a stage that needs redesigning to first-principles-thinking, and a constraint that is another actor's choice to game-theory-for-decisions.

### psychological-profiling

- **Owns:** an evidence-graded profile of a person built from their own public words and record: what they want, how they decide, how they talk, what to say and what to avoid — every claim carrying its evidence, a confidence level, and the thing that would prove it wrong.

- **Triggers:** a first outreach or an important meeting with someone not yet known; negotiation or partnership prep where the other person's drivers decide the outcome.

- **Consumes:** the person's public record — posts, interviews, talks, filings — and the purpose of the meeting or outreach.

- **Hands off:** the dossier, with say and avoid guidance, to cold-outreach, fundraising, or game-theory-for-decisions. When the question is whether the person's claims are true rather than what drives them, that is due-diligence.

## Decide

### game-theory-for-decisions

- **Owns:** decisions whose outcome depends on other actors' choices: mapping the players, their payoffs, the sequence, and the information, then choosing the best move — or changing the game.

- **Triggers:** a negotiation, bid, offer, or deadline; a fundraising, sale, licensing, or partnership process with several parties; a competitor's launch, price move, or market entry; a platform or policy change users will adapt to; a coalition problem — who must agree, who can block.

- **Consumes:** evidence about the players — what they did, what they said, what their role rewards, what constrains them — each fact labeled observed, declared, inferred, or unknown.

- **Hands off:** a verdict the decision owner can act on, the player and payoff maps, the likely outcome with its assumptions, the best response, the game-changing move, the move not to make, and a response plan with its information calendar. Calls fundraising when the game is a raise or a priced negotiation.

### fundraising

- **Owns:** raising capital, and selling a business, run as one shared competitive process — pipeline, always-be-closing discipline, the lead or anchor investor, the second offer, the deadline close — with track-specific playbooks for startup seed and venture rounds, GP fund raises from LPs, and company M&A or strategic exits.

- **Triggers:** a seed or venture round being planned or run; a fund raise from limited partners; a company sale or strategic exit; any priced negotiation the game-theory run hands it.

- **Consumes:** a finished position — the concept comes from the positioning record and is borrowed whole, never re-set inside the raise.

- **Hands off:** the raise run as a process: the pipeline, the sequencing of investors, the anchor strategy, the terms conversation, and the close. Its drafts carry the fundraising guardrails wherever the router's merged output quotes them.

### due-diligence

- **Owns:** checking what is verifiably true about a person or a company before committing to them: identity, entities, legal and regulatory history, financial-distress signals, and whether their claims match the public record. Every item returns with a status, a source, and a date.

- **Triggers:** a partnership, an investment, a hire, or a major contract being considered; a counterparty whose claims need checking against the record before money, signature, or reputation moves.

- **Consumes:** the subject's name and identifiers, the claims to check, and the decision the check feeds.

- **Hands off:** the verified fact sheet — which feeds psychological-profiling when the subject is a person — and the evidence base for a commit or do-not-commit decision.

## Position

### positioning

- **Owns:** what a company or product will be thought of as — decided in a fixed order: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value with proof, best-fit customers, market category last — and passed through a strategy gate of named trade-offs and a parity-versus-difference test.

- **Triggers:** before a launch; a repositioning; a new segment or market; the symptom that the pitch keeps changing; growth stalling at the same ceiling because the business is invisible in the buying situations that matter.

- **Consumes:** buyer evidence — win and loss accounts, what customers would do instead, the language happy customers use. Where the evidence is thin, the run says so at the step it thins.

- **Hands off:** the positioning record — owned concept, market category, competitive frame, best-fit customers, trade-offs, plus the supporting record — written to be consumed directly as the message square of one-page-marketing-plan and as the position fields of a funnel-operator config. Calls first-principles-thinking at the evidence step and comms at the statement step.

### zero-to-one

- **Owns:** whether a venture is a creation or a copy, tested on Peter Thiel's frame: the creation verdict read off a decomposition of the claimed novelty; the secret, stated in one sentence and tested three ways; the plan rewritten definite, with the rest named as bets; the monopoly read, with Girard's mimetic desire as the lens for why markets crowd; the seven questions answered or marked unanswered; and the beachhead sequence, small market dominated first.

- **Triggers:** a founding or a pivot being weighed; serious money about to follow a plan — a raise, an investment, a year of a team's life; a crowded market being read as validation; a strategy written in the language of optionality.

- **Consumes:** the plan as written, the venture's numbers where they exist, and the market's facts — entry, margins, how rivals describe their own reasons. What cannot be evidenced is marked unanswered, which is information too.

- **Hands off:** the zero-to-one record — verdict, secret with its disprover, definite claim and bets, monopoly and mimetic reads, the seven answers, the beachhead sequence, and the re-run triggers. After a creation verdict it hands off to business-model-canvas for the model record and to positioning for what the new thing will be thought of as. Calls first-principles-thinking at the verdict, game-theory-for-decisions at the monopoly read, and fundraising when the run feeds a raise.

### business-model-canvas

- **Owns:** how a business makes money, stated and tested on one sheet: a fit test picks the Lean Canvas while the model is still being searched for, or the nine-block Business Model Canvas once it is found; the sheet is filled from evidence and finished with the riskiest assumptions, ranked, each with its next test.

- **Triggers:** a business whose model is unstated, untested, or being redrawn; a marketing plan whose economics have no canvas under them; a pivot being weighed.

- **Consumes:** what is known about the business — customers, offer, channels, costs, revenue — with each block's evidence labeled.

- **Hands off:** the filled sheet, the value-proposition zoom, the environment check the sheet omits, and the ranked list of riskiest assumptions with their tests. A visual render of the canvas is part of its deliverable where the skill's own contract requires one.

### vision-and-manifesto

- **Owns:** a vision, a mission, or a manifesto that does work: one core set of beliefs written at three lengths — the vision as a picturable future scene with a quantifiable objective and milestones, the mission as the work now, and a manifesto only when a named public job exists for one — finished only when the statements pass a decision gate: they change real decisions about hiring, firing, and money.

- **Triggers:** a founder or leadership team setting or resetting direction; statements that exist but decide nothing; a manifesto being considered for a public moment.

- **Consumes:** the core beliefs and the measured vision research the skill is built on; the position, where one exists, so the statements and the market story do not drift apart.

- **Hands off:** the statements at three lengths, with the decision-gate result. Drafts that fail the gate are reported as drafts, not shipped as vision.

## Plan

### one-page-marketing-plan

- **Owns:** the whole marketing system on one page — who it is for, what it says, where it reaches people, how leads are caught, kept, and converted, and what happens after the sale — built diagnosis first, then Allan Dib's nine squares in order, and finished with the brand-versus-activation split stated as a hypothesis the business's own numbers will judge.

- **Triggers:** a business needs its marketing system stated on one page; an existing plan is a tactic pile; a positioning record exists and needs carrying to market; a budget decision between building future demand and harvesting present demand.

- **Consumes:** a positioning record, whole — its chain-out block becomes the plan's message square with no re-derivation. Where none exists, a light message step runs and the plan is marked as carrying it.

- **Hands off:** the diagnosis and guiding policy, the nine squares, the split line with its review date, and the standards and measures each square will be checked against. Calls positioning at its first squares, stop-the-scroll at its creative, and business-model-canvas at its economics.

### cold-outreach

- **Owns:** first messages to people with no prior relationship — cold email and cold DM — and the judging of them: the offer tested first, the batch built from a trigger, the warm path and the history checked before anyone is written to, one true observation researched per recipient, and a short sequence whose first ask is a question about interest.

- **Triggers:** a cold email or DM to write; a sequence to plan; an outreach draft to judge before it sends.

- **Consumes:** the offer, the target list and its trigger events, the sender's history with each recipient, and the position the message must stay inside.

- **Hands off:** the sequence, written to send, with every rule it followed labeled by the evidence behind it. What it hands off is a draft for approval — sending follows the owner's standing rules, never the router's momentum.

### funnel-operator

- **Owns:** a video channel run as a system rather than as one-off uploads: the channel set up once as a single config file, then every episode produced through the same nine stages — topic engine, script, storyboard, assets, voice and music, assembly, packaging, publish gate, numbers read — so a second channel costs a new config, not a new method.

- **Triggers:** a channel starting; a channel that exists but runs on memory; a second or third channel wanted; episodes produced by an agent, where the work must be checkable.

- **Consumes:** a positioning record, consumed whole into the channel config; the marketing plan's media and capture squares, which the config's funnel endpoint must match; the channel folder's backlog, style bible, and ledgers.

- **Hands off:** an episode package — script with sources, storyboard, final cut uploaded unlisted, three title options, thumbnail, description — plus the channel state updates. Its publish gate binds every run it joins: nothing publishes without the channel owner's approval of the exact final video, title, and thumbnail. Calls storytelling at the script and storyboard stages, comms at script and packaging, first-principles-thinking and game-theory-for-decisions at setup.

## Say

### comms

- **Owns:** public moments and how a thing is said — announcements, launches, crises, responses, and the everyday content that decides whether anyone is listening when the moments come. The story is the founder's job: this skill names the moment, sorts the claims by who can say them, builds the narrative, and runs the plain-writing gate.

- **Triggers:** anything the public will hear — a launch, a raise announcement, a result, an attack or failure that needs an answer, a post or video that carries the position.

- **Consumes:** a finished position. Comms says the position; it does not set it. A moment that needs a different position to work sends the run back to positioning.

- **Hands off:** the moment named in one sentence, the claim sort, the statement written to ship, and the response plan. Calls stop-the-scroll when the message must stop a feed, and write-in-your-voice when the message must sound like a named person.

### write-in-your-voice

- **Owns:** a working voice for a person, built from how they actually speak — a tiered source bank assembled from recordings, transcripts, and dictations — and drafting in that voice, gated on the draft's shape, not just its words.

- **Triggers:** setting up AI drafting for a founder, creator, or executive; drafts that keep coming back "not my voice"; a voice profile built from published posts that is not working.

- **Consumes:** the person's speech — recordings, transcripts, dictations. Published posts alone are a known failure mode this skill exists to correct.

- **Hands off:** the voice profile and drafts that pass its shape gate. Inside a router run, it governs any passage that must sound like the named person, under comms' direction for the moment.

### stop-the-scroll

- **Owns:** whether a piece of content earns attention: AIDA with the ends hardened — the hook and the call to action each built to stand out in a sea of a hundred and to stand alone with full context — over a universal, impact-led middle.

- **Triggers:** a post, a thread, a launch, a script, or an outline that must stop a feed and hold a stranger; creative inside a marketing plan's media square.

- **Consumes:** the content and the position it carries. It hardens the ends of a piece whose middle and message already exist.

- **Hands off:** the hook and the call to action, rebuilt to stand alone, and the middle checked for impact. It does not set the message — that is the position's and comms' work upstream of it.

### storytelling

- **Owns:** narrative structure — the hook, the shape of the turn, and scene function: every scene must advance, reveal, or reverse, or it is cut. Its lessons are labeled by the evidence under them: measured research first, large-sample practitioner knowledge second, famous frameworks held as framing only, and the channel's or publication's own retention data as the court of appeal that rewrites lessons over time.

- **Triggers:** a script or storyboard being shaped; a story-shaped piece of content — a video, a talk, a launch narrative — whose structure will decide whether anyone stays.

- **Consumes:** the material to be shaped, the format's constraints (length, chapter rhythm, voice), and the position the story must carry.

- **Hands off:** the story structure — arc, hook, chapter turns, scene functions — delivered as scene cards a producer can build from, which funnel-operator builds episodes on and comms builds moments on. It owns the shape of the telling, never the facts being told: claims still trace to their sources under the consuming skill's rules.

### mascot-system

- **Owns:** recurring-character illustration — character bibles, panel grammar, chained generation, and the drift check that keeps one character recognisably the same across every image. It produces images only: headlines, captions, and the signature block are text layers behind flags (`headline`, `caption`, `signature`), all off by default and set by the caller.

- **Triggers:** any piece that must show a recurring character consistently — carousels, decks, character-led video, a mascot carried across a launch.

- **Consumes:** the piece's panel list or storytelling's scene cards, the series' character bible with its reference anchors, and the caller's text-layer flags for the job.

- **Hands off:** the finished images in order, a drift-check result per panel, and any bible updates the run made. Channel-operator calls it at its assets stage when a channel's style bible names a character system.

### interview-yourself

- **Owns:** content drawn from a person's own lived experience by interview: an interviewer that asks questions a stranger already cares about, with the receipt drawn from the subject's own week and the numbers researched and attached before asking, so the subject answers out loud in about thirty seconds and the answers become the content.

- **Triggers:** a launch — launch content is built from the subject's interviewed answers, never from a brief alone; a founder, expert, or operator with more experience than time to write; a blank page that will not move.

- **Consumes:** the subject's week and work as the receipt source, the skill or topic being launched, and researched numbers attached to each prompt before it is asked.

- **Hands off:** the interview prompts, teed up with their context, and — once the subject answers — the raw material that comms and write-in-your-voice shape into posts and pages. Inside a router run it is a stage that waits on a human: the run prepares the prompts and marks the content steps behind them as pending the answers.

### podcast-yourself

- **Owns:** the prep of a single talking-head video: mining the performer's counted content results into winning and losing shapes, sorting every claim the video wants to make, and building the sequential riff sheet — one question per beat, the receipt to hit, the line to land, the cold-open flags — plus the edit brief the footage is later cut under.

- **Triggers:** a launch, announcement, or result being filmed as a talking-head video; a long video (twenty minutes or more) that a topic list cannot hold; a performer with a content record whose results can be mined; footage planned for a retention edit — sound bites up front, beats reordered.

- **Consumes:** the video's job in one sentence with its one action, the performer's recent content counts from a named source over a stated window, and the documented receipts behind the claims.

- **Hands off:** the riff sheet (beats in filming order, four lines each), the edit brief in its fixed five-step order, and the evidence note with the counts, the claim sort, and the cut list. Calls stop-the-scroll at its mining step, comms at its claim sort, storytelling at its beat order, and fundraising at its invitation beat. Boundary: interview-yourself draws material out by interview when none exists yet; funnel-operator runs a channel as a system, where this skill preps one video.

## The router itself

### playbook

- **Owns:** selection, order, and merge across this roster — and the closing line that proves a run happened.

- **Triggers:** the phrase "run the playbook" and its plain equivalents; any substantive task handed over with no skills named; a task whose shape matches several roster triggers at once.

- **Consumes:** the task, named in one sentence with its deliverable and success outcome.

- **Hands off:** one merged deliverable, the playbook line, and the working notes. It adds no judgment of its own inside any stage; the selected skill's method governs there.

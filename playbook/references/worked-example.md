# Worked example — one decision, six skills

A real run of the router, from November 2026, with the private details generalized. It is included because it shows the three things a reader most needs to see: how selection works on a task that names no skills, how the fixed order makes later skills consume earlier ones, and what the merged output and the playbook line look like when the run is done.

## The task as it arrived

The owner runs two businesses that could each support a video channel. One is a business-to-business services firm: few possible customers, each worth a great deal, with documented referral terms already in place. The other is a consumer health brand: a large possible audience, and nothing to sell yet — no product, no paid offer, only future ad revenue. The owner's ask, in full, was: run the relevant skills on which channel to start with, for monetizing and growing — and then plan how one reusable skill could run a whole channel, so more channels can follow.

No skills were named. That is the router's home ground.

Step 1 named the task: **decide which of two channels to build first, measured by expected dollars per finished video and months to the first dollar — and produce the plan for the channel-running skill.** The unit is one finished video. That unit matters: it is what lets the two options be compared on one scale at all.

## The selection

The consideration list, as recorded in the run's working notes:

- **first-principles-thinking — ran.** The decision turned on a price nobody had decomposed: what one video is worth under each model.

- **fix-the-bottleneck — ran.** Both options are chains from video to cash, and the two chains fail in different places. That is this skill's exact shape.

- **game-theory-for-decisions — ran.** A platform sets the rules and has sanctioned the owner's other channel before; competitors crowd one field and are absent from the other. Other actors' choices gate the outcome.

- **fundraising — skipped.** No capital is being raised and no business is being sold in this task.

- **positioning — ran.** Each channel needed its own record before either could be ranked, and one of them had never had its whole-product gate tested.

- **business-model-canvas — skipped.** Both models were already stated plainly enough for the decision; no block of either model was the disputed question. Noted as the first skill to add if the winner's economics get challenged.

- **vision-and-manifesto — skipped.** No direction statement was in play.

- **one-page-marketing-plan — ran.** The winning channel needed its system stated — target, message, capture, split — before the skill plan built on it.

- **cold-outreach — skipped.** No first messages were being written in this run. The winner's outreach already existed as its own plan.

- **funnel-operator — not yet.** It did not exist when the run started. The run's second half designed it. This is the router working as intended: a gap in the roster was named, not papered over, and the missing skill was built as a separate job.

- **comms — ran.** The verdict had to be stated the way the owner would repeat it: plainly, in one breath, with the numbers spoken.

- **write-in-your-voice, stop-the-scroll, storytelling, interview-yourself — skipped.** No public post, launch, or script was being produced in this run. They run later, when the channel's first episodes are made.

Six skills ran. Nine were considered and skipped, each with a reason. The skips are half the value of the run: they are what stops the next reader from asking why the obvious-looking skill never fired.

## The run, in order

**Understand — first-principles-thinking.** The decomposition stripped four assumptions, the load-bearing one being "a channel makes money from ads." Under the services model that is false: the money sits behind the video, in documented referral bounties — one converted company pays a five-figure bounty at the documented low end, and more at the top. Under the health model it is true, and the floor math is unforgiving: ad rates in the category run to a few hundred dollars for a strong early video, payable only after months of qualifying for the platform's partner program. The expected value per video came out roughly a hundred to one in the services channel's favor at the low end. The verdict was set here; everything after this step tested or carried it.

**Understand — fix-the-bottleneck.** Each option was mapped as a chain: video, viewer, click, screening, interview, qualified customer, cash. The services chain has one narrow stage — the screening funnel behind the videos is not built yet — and a narrow stage can be relieved. The health chain has a missing stage: there is nothing to convert into, and a chain with a hole passes zero however much flows in at the top. The bottleneck read agreed with the floor math and added the sequencing: build the compounding asset (the video library) while the funnel gets built, because the library costs the constraint nothing.

**Decide — game-theory-for-decisions.** Three players could change the outcome. The platform: it had already suspended the owner's flagship channel from its partner program under a policy about AI personas on sensitive topics — and health is a sensitive topic, so the health channel replays that game on its hardest setting. Competitors: the health field is an arms race of giant human creators and thousands of AI channels, while the services channel's category is unclaimed on the platform — the first mover names it. The audience: the owner's existing followers skew toward founders, so the services channel can be seeded and the health channel starts cold. The game-changing move: claim the empty category while claiming it is free.

**Position — positioning.** Light records were run for both channels, alternatives first and category last. The services channel's record passed the strategy gate: the whole product is present for its audience today — the screening, the report offer, and the referral routes all exist — so the position can be cashed. The health channel's record failed the same gate: its position promises guidance toward products and services that do not exist yet. It was parked, not ranked lower — parked, with the condition that would unpark it stated in the record: an offer.

**Plan — one-page-marketing-plan.** The winning channel's system was stated on one page. Diagnosis: a proved offer, documented terms, and named buyers, with no owned reach — no founder has ever seen the firm think in public. Guiding policy: build the library that makes the firm the obvious answer at the moment a founder first wonders what their asset is worth, at the price of publishing nothing off-topic, ever. The message square consumed the positioning record whole. The split was stated at the business-to-business starting hypothesis, held as a hypothesis, with its review trigger set at the fiftieth video.

**Say — comms.** The merged output was written the way the owner would say it: the verdict first, the math in spoken numbers, one idea per line. Comms contributed no new analysis — that is correct behavior for the last skill in the order. Its job was that the decision could be repeated by the owner in one breath without losing its spine.

## The merged output

One document. The verdict at the top, in two sentences. Then the decomposition, the two chain maps, the game read, both positioning records, and the winner's one-page plan — each section in its own skill's output shape, each figure carrying its label (documented, observed, estimated). Then the second half: the design of the channel-running skill the owner had asked for — the method in the skill, each channel as one config file, the nine production stages, the publish gate, and the build sequence. Conflicts: none arose that a boundary did not settle — positioning's gate result (one record parked) stood over the game read's softer phrasing of the same point, because the gate is positioning's to run.

The closing line of that document, in the router's shape:

> **Playbook:** first-principles-thinking (the floor math that set the verdict, roughly a hundred to one) → fix-the-bottleneck (a narrow stage versus a missing one; the library builds while the funnel is built) → game-theory-for-decisions (the platform had already sanctioned the sensitive-topic shape; the other category was unclaimed) → positioning (the services record passed the gate; the health record parked for want of an offer) → one-page-marketing-plan (the winner's system on one page, split held as a hypothesis) → comms (the verdict in the owner's own spoken form). Skipped, with reasons in the working notes: fundraising, business-model-canvas, vision-and-manifesto, cold-outreach, write-in-your-voice, stop-the-scroll, storytelling, interview-yourself. Roster gap named: funnel-operator, built afterward as its own job.

## What the run shows

- **The order earned its keep twice.** Positioning's gate parked the health channel on a test the floor math alone would never have run — the two skills agreed, for different reasons, which is what made the verdict safe to act on. And the plan consumed the positioning record instead of re-deriving a message, so the document never argues with itself.

- **The most valuable output was a skip.** Channel-operator not existing was the run's biggest finding. A router that improvises missing skills would have hidden it. This one named it, and the collection grew.

- **Nothing in the merged output rests on a skill that was only named.** Every section can be traced to a procedure that actually ran — the decomposition's assumption table, the bottleneck run's chain maps, the gate results. That traceability is the whole product.

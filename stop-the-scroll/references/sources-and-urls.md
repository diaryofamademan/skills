# Sources and credits

| Source | What this skill takes from it |
| --- | --- |
| E. St. Elmo Lewis, advertising writings, c. 1898 | The original funnel formulation (attract attention, maintain interest, create desire) that became AIDA |
| E. K. Strong Jr., *The Psychology of Selling and Advertising*, McGraw-Hill, 1925 | AIDA set down as a named framework: attention, interest, desire, action |
| The author's launch session, November 2026 (fourteen versions of one launch, X and LinkedIn) | The thirteen principles: the hardening of AIDA's ends, the universal and impact-led middle, the formatting rule, and the direction-is-not-copy guardrail. Primary source; the worked record is `case-study-bottleneck-launch.md` |

AIDA is a century old and public property as a framework; the contribution here is not the frame but the standards applied to its ends and the drafting discipline around them, all of it earned in the session above. No source text is reproduced beyond short attributed formulations.

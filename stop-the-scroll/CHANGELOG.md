# Changelog

## 0.2.4 — 2026-11-16

Stop-the-scroll pass over the README. The opening now starts from the reader's question instead of describing the skill, and the README closes with a Get the skill block pointing to the skill's page on ianbalina.com, where the email form lives. No method changes.

## 0.2.3 — 2026-11-16

Public copy rewritten in plain spoken words; no method changes. The README opening, the marketplace description, and the listing lines were rewritten so a stranger can follow every line: short sentences, no compressed chains, no labels that assume context the reader does not have. Part of the eight-skill plain-copy pass Ian directed on 2026-11-16, after the skill pages were found to read as robotic.

## 0.2.2 — 2026-11-16

The spoken-numbers rule joins principle 5 and the verification checklist: every number is written the way a person says it out loud, rounded to the spoken form ("more than 304,000 emails," "$307 billion," "about 3 in 4"); a raw precise count (304,174) or a decimal abbreviation ($307.0B) fails. The stats rule already demanded one visible number and no reader math, but it never demanded the spoken form, and finished copy printed a raw count of 304,174 behind it. Exact figures live in the source notes; the copy carries the number as speech. No other changes.

## 0.2.1 — 2026-11-16

A grammar guardrail (guardrail 12) and a matching checklist item: no piece ships without a grammar pass on the exact final text — subject-verb agreement, articles, punctuation, hyphenation, parallelism. The thirteen principles judge whether a piece earns attention; none of them judged whether it is correct, and finished copy passed every principle carrying a plain agreement error. Correctness is now a gate beside them, not a cleanup after them. No other changes.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-15

Six rules from the fix-the-bottleneck launch's later rounds (the results post went through about six more versions after 0.1.7), approved as a batch: (1) use the source's own result, not a summary of it; (2) never write a formula out as a sentence; (3) simple math shown as math is a clarity tool — three numbers, named rates, round percentages ("26 × 25% completion rate × 50% ship rate = 3 tasks shipped") — refining the no-reader-math test to ban only math the reader has to work at; (4) one everyday word per stage of a flow, held constant at every mention across every post; (5) lead with in vs out when there is a leak; (6) show smart actors missing the whole picture, each locally right and globally blind, instead of reporting the failure flatly. Plus a patterns addition: when one post carries the argument, draft it three or four complete ways for the author to pick. The launch's final results post is the worked example in the patterns reference.

## 0.1.7 — 2026-11-15

The callback rule corrected, on the author's word minutes after 0.1.6: the metaphor is the whole image (the tap, the water, the hose, and the kink), not the single striking word. Callbacks draw on the metaphor's full world, and at least one beat states the mapping plainly (the system is the hose; the bottleneck is its kink). The worked examples in the procedure and the patterns reference are updated to the corrected lines. No other changes.

## 0.1.6 — 2026-11-15

The callback rule added to the thread architecture: when the story sets a metaphor, the method line, the big win, and the call to action each call back to it by name at least once, in the story's vocabulary, rather than switching back to the abstract term. Worked examples from the founding launch's kinked hose are recorded in the patterns reference. No other changes.

## 0.1.5 — 2026-11-15

Fifth move added to the story-punch treatment: stakes. A deadline, a cost, or something at risk, stated in the setup, so the story has something that can be lost. Earned on the corrected Herbie draft, which was better but not yet punchy; a clock ("make camp by dark" ... "and they make camp in time") made the same facts read tenser. No other changes.

## 0.1.4 — 2026-11-15

The story's third test added: the logic pass. Every physical claim in a story post must hold together under a plain reading. Earned by the author's catch on the punchy hike draft, which placed Herbie at the back of the line and then claimed nobody could pass him; the worked example in the patterns reference is corrected to the book's own mechanics (Herbie walks in the middle; the troop arrives when he arrives). The pass is stated in the procedure, the principles file, and the checklist. No other changes.

## 0.1.3 — 2026-11-15

The sea-of-100 test extended to the story post, from the same launch's analogy pass: the story must stand out in a sea of 100 stories, and a boring story is raw material to make exciting, not an excuse. The four-part treatment is recorded in the procedure, the checklist, and the patterns reference (name the character, one concrete detail, the stuck beat on its own short line, the backwards turn ending on an image), worked on the hike story from The Goal. ## 0.1.2 — 2026-11-15

The stand-alone rule extended from the hook and the call to action to every load-bearing post, the story explicitly, from the author's analogy pass on the fix-the-bottleneck launch: a story post that leans on the thread for its setup is rewritten with the setup inside the post. ## 0.1.1 — 2026-11-14

Principle 5 sharpened with the stats rule, from the author's statistic pick for the fix-the-bottleneck launch the same night: a statistic must multiply or reduce (increase money, increase efficiency, reduce waste, increase speed) and pass three tests — one visible number, no reader math, and a change shown rather than a state. The rejected and winning candidate statistics are recorded as the worked examples in references/the-13-principles.md. No other changes.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-14

First build. Published briefly as one-in-a-hundred and renamed to stop-the-scroll the same night at the author's direction, before any launch: the name should state what the skill does to the reader, not the odds it beats. AIDA with the ends hardened: thirteen principles in five groups (hook, body, call to action, formatting, drafting discipline), two tests applied to each end (sea-of-100 and stand-alone), an output contract for build and review runs, and the worked case study of the fourteen-version launch session the principles were distilled from. Boundaries against the voice skills (sound) and the comms skill (communications decisions) are stated in the limits section.

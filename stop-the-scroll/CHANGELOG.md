# Changelog

## 0.4.0 — 2026-11-21

Claude Hopkins joins the skill as its testing backbone. The thirteen principles and the two tests judge a piece before it ships; nothing in the skill judged it after, and winners were picked by taste wherever no count existed. *Scientific Advertising* (1923) supplies the missing half.

- **A testing backbone section.** Every hook and call to action that ships is keyed, so its result can be counted and set against the variations it beat. Counted results outrank taste: where returns exist, the variation round is judged against them first, and a winner picked against the counts needs a recorded reason. New ends test small before they spend big. And a claim runs only if it can be tracked to a result — specific, checkable claims over general ones, because they test better and can be counted.

- **Wired into the procedure, not bolted on.** Guardrail 13 states the keying rule beside the construction rules. Step 4 gains the counts bullet, so the round reads earlier returns before judging. A build run now returns a fifth item, the key record, and the verification checklist gains the matching item.

- **The label and the boundary, stated in the skill.** Hopkins is a practitioner method from 1923, before the controlled-study era; his results are his own reports from his own campaigns, and the Sources section and the credit table say so. He is overruled by measured evidence wherever the two disagree, the same standing Ries, Trout, and Schwartz carry in the strategy skills. His era's patent-medicine claim style does not enter: the import is the testing method, not his copy examples, and anything conflicting with the author's claims discipline stays out. He is not a fourteenth principle — the thirteen judge construction, Hopkins judges results, and the skill keeps the two judgments separate.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-11-19

The variation round joins the core procedure as step 4. Every post in a piece — the hook, every load-bearing middle post, and the call to action — gets 10 complete variations, each a whole post built from a different angle, not a word swap. Every variation is judged against the sea-of-100 test and the stand-alone test, the piece is built from the per-post winners, and the reason each winner won is recorded in the build notes so the judgment can be audited. The ends are locked only after the round. Steps 5 and 6 are the old steps 4 and 5, unchanged. The change came from a launch build that drafted one hook per post and kept the first that passed: a single draft was never tested against the sea.

## 0.2.5 — 2026-11-19

What a run returns now calls in the writing pass. When a build or review run ships in raw workshop register, the tests and scores drown the copy itself, and a review reads as a checklist instead of a verdict the author can act on. The pass runs the work-humanizer skill in rewrite mode over the piece in its final form and over the run's returned verdicts and review report, then applies the writing-styles skill's plain rules, and neither is done until the pass has run on the exact text being delivered. Facts, figures, quotes, names, dates, and statuses never change in the pass.

## 0.2.2 — 2026-11-16

The spoken-numbers rule joins principle 5 and the verification checklist: every number is written the way a person says it out loud, rounded to the spoken form ("more than 304,000 emails," "$307 billion," "about 3 in 4"); a raw precise count (304,174) or a decimal abbreviation ($307.0B) fails. The stats rule already demanded one visible number and no reader math, but it never demanded the spoken form, and finished copy printed a raw count of 304,174 behind it. Exact figures live in the source notes; the copy carries the number as speech. No other changes.

## 0.2.1 — 2026-11-16

A grammar guardrail (guardrail 12) and a matching checklist item: no piece ships without a grammar pass on the exact final text — subject-verb agreement, articles, punctuation, hyphenation, parallelism. The thirteen principles judge whether a piece earns attention; none of them judged whether it is correct, and finished copy passed every principle carrying a plain agreement error. Correctness is now a gate beside them, not a cleanup after them. No other changes.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-15

Six rules from the fix-the-bottleneck launch's later rounds (the results post went through about six more versions after 0.1.7), approved as a batch: (1) use the source's own result, not a summary of it; (2) never write a formula out as a sentence; (3) simple math shown as math is a clarity tool — three numbers, named rates, round percentages ("26 × 25% completion rate × 50% ship rate = 3 tasks shipped") — refining the no-reader-math test to ban only math the reader has to work at; (4) one everyday word per stage of a flow, held constant at every mention across every post; (5) lead with in vs out when there is a leak; (6) show smart actors missing the whole picture, each locally right and globally blind, instead of reporting the failure flatly. Plus a patterns addition: when one post carries the argument, draft it three or four complete ways for the author to pick. The launch's final results post is the worked example in the patterns reference.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-14

First build. Published briefly as one-in-a-hundred and renamed to stop-the-scroll the same night at the author's direction, before any launch: the name should state what the skill does to the reader, not the odds it beats. AIDA with the ends hardened: thirteen principles in five groups (hook, body, call to action, formatting, drafting discipline), two tests applied to each end (sea-of-100 and stand-alone), an output contract for build and review runs, and the worked case study of the fourteen-version launch session the principles were distilled from.

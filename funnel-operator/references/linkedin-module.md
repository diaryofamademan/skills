# The LinkedIn module — posts and document carousels

LinkedIn is the channel's professional distribution surface: the platform where the channel's audience, in most business categories, already sits in its working identity. The module is optional per channel — a channel runs it when its config's Distribution block marks LinkedIn active. Its job is the Shorts' job on a different surface: put the episode's sharpest claim in front of people who would never click the video, in the formats this platform rewards, and route them to the episode and the funnel endpoint behind it.

## What gets published

- **Posts.** The episode's core claim re-cut as a self-contained text post: the claim first, the reasoning in short lines, and one pointer at the end — to the episode, or straight to the funnel endpoint where the config says the surface points there. A post is written for LinkedIn, not copied from the video description: the description's job is to sit under a video, and a post that reads like one travels like one.

- **Document carousels.** The episode's chapter points or cards, built as a document post — a small deck the reader swipes through, one idea per page, the last page carrying the pointer. Carousels are built from the episode's own materials (its cards, its stills, its chapter structure), so the surface costs layout work, not new production.

Both are prepared at the packaging stage (stage 7), alongside the title and thumbnail, so the owner approves the episode's whole public face in one package.

## The posting routes — stated honestly

- **The browser route is the default.** LinkedIn's member-posting API is partner-gated: posting as a member through the API requires access granted through LinkedIn's developer programs, which most channels will not hold and cannot simply sign up for. So the working route for a personal profile is the browser — the post is made through the signed-in profile (or Page) in a browser session, exactly as the account's owner would make it.

- **The API route exists only where a channel legitimately has it.** A channel whose organisation holds approved API access with posting permission may post through it, and records that in its Distribution block. An agent never assumes API access, never applies for partner access in the channel's name without the owner's word, and never treats a read-only or analytics token as posting permission.

- **The scheduler route.** A channel may post through a scheduler it already runs. Two are named: Typefully, which takes one draft and posts it to LinkedIn and X together; and Postiz, this operation's chosen default (owner's decision, 2026-11-25 — one tool across LinkedIn, X, Instagram, and YouTube, driven through its API; LinkedIn carousels are built there from the carousel's page images rather than a finished PDF). The route changes the hand that posts, never the discipline around it: the gate approves the exact copy and the exact media first, the scheduler holds only approved artefacts, and after the scheduled post goes live it is read back on LinkedIn itself, exactly as a browser post is — the scheduler's own "published" mark is not the verification.

- **The identity is a config decision.** Posting as the owner's personal profile and posting as a Company Page are different voices with different reach, and the block records which one the channel uses. The module does not choose for the channel.

## The gate

Distribution rides the episode's publish gate (stage 8), under the same rule as everything else the channel publishes: **the owner approves the exact copy and the exact media before anything posts.** The gate package carries the post text word for word and the carousel document itself, page by page — not a summary of them. Nothing posts silently, nothing posts on a "close enough" approval of a draft, and a post changed after approval in any way goes back through the gate. Posts go out in stage 8's post-publish pass, after the episode is public, so the pointer they carry lands on a live episode. A standalone post not tied to an episode follows the same rule with its own package: exact copy, exact media, the owner's word, then posting.

## Verification

A post is not done when it is submitted; it is done when it is read back. Open the live post and confirm: the text as approved, the carousel's pages in order and legible, and the pointer's link working and landing where it should. The post's URL is recorded on the episode's line in the published log, beside the episode's other surfaces. A post that went out wrong is corrected or removed in the same pass and the correction noted — never left standing because fixing it is a second trip.

## The numbers read

Stage 9 reads LinkedIn the way it reads Shorts and blog posts: on movement, not applause. Post views and reactions are context; the measures are profile and page visits the posts produce, clicks through to the episode, and the funnel starts and completions that follow them, reported beside the episode's numbers and never blended into them.

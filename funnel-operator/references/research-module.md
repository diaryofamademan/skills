# The research module — nothing is created blind

The topic backlog carries three scores per topic: search demand, story strength, funnel fit. Scores written from judgment alone are guesses wearing numbers' clothes — plausible, confident, and unconnected to anything the market has actually done. That is the failure this module exists to prevent: **scoring topics on judgment alone.** The module is the channel's standing market and content research. It runs before the backlog is first scored, again whenever stage 1 picks or scores topics, and again at every weekly numbers read, so the backlog is ranked on evidence that stays current instead of on what somebody believed the week the channel launched.

The module works inside guardrail 7, not around it. Topics still come from the channel's well — the audience's questions, the business's own records in anonymised shape, published buyer or reader criteria, the owner's inbox objections. Research does not import topics the well cannot support. What it does is measure: which of the well's questions the audience is demonstrably asking, in the words they demonstrably use, which treatments of those questions the market is demonstrably rewarding, and what the channel's buyers are demonstrably paying for. Funnel fit stays a veto throughout — a topic the research shows performing everywhere still does not get made if it cannot end at the channel's funnel endpoint.

## When it runs

- **At setup, before the backlog is seeded.** The first research pass runs before the ten seed topics are scored, so the channel's first ranking already rests on evidence.

- **At stage 1, before a pick is scored or re-scored.** A topic whose scores predate the last research pass is re-scored against the current findings before it is picked.

- **At every weekly numbers read.** The scoreboard module's weekly review runs the refresh pass below: findings are updated, the backlog is re-ranked, and the refill rule is checked. The daily brief does not run research; it reads the channel's own numbers.

## The four passes

**What is performing on the video platform, in the channel's niche.** Search the channel's entry-point questions — the queries its audience types, from the config — and read what the platform returns: which videos surface, how their view counts sit against their channels' sizes, which formats and hooks are pulling, how often the strong channels publish. Competitor and neighbour channels are read directly, not through summaries: their recent uploads, their strongest episodes, the packaging those episodes carry. The finding recorded is never "this topic is hot"; it is the specific shape that is working — the question as phrased, the format it was answered in, the hook its title and thumbnail used — because a shape can be tested against the channel's own position and a temperature cannot.

**Search demand, in the audience's own words.** The terms the audience actually uses, gathered from the platform's own search suggestions for the entry-point questions, from search-engine results and any keyword tool the channel legitimately has access to, and from the questions as they are phrased in the places the audience asks them. The output is a term list with the audience's phrasing preserved word for word: titles, scripts, and descriptions are written in the words people search, not in the words the business uses internally. A volume figure from a third-party tool is an estimate and is recorded as one, with its source named; a suggestion the platform itself offers is direct evidence the query is typed, and outranks any tool's estimate.

**What is performing on the channel's other platforms, in the niche.** For each platform the channel's Distribution block marks active — and for the platforms its audience demonstrably uses, whether the channel publishes there yet or not — the same read: the accounts in the niche, the posts that travelled, and the formats doing the travelling (on LinkedIn, document carousels and plain-text posts; on X, threads and long-form posts; on Instagram, reels and carousels). This pass feeds two places: the backlog, where a question performing on a text platform is evidence for its search-demand and story scores, and the distribution modules, where the formats that travel are the formats the channel's own posts are built in.

**Buyer and market demand signals.** What the buyers in the channel's market are paying for, want more of, and have stopped buying. The channel's own deal flow and market sources come first — the business's buyer conversations, the approaches it receives, the criteria its buyers publish, the deals it can see from inside its own market — because a buyer's stated criteria in a real negotiation outrank any public commentary about the market. Public market reporting, trade coverage, and buyer-side publishing come second, as corroboration and range. A demand claim enters the findings only with a source named in the channel folder, under the same discipline as guardrail 2: no named source, no claim. Where the market's money and the audience's attention point at different topics, both findings are recorded as they stand; the tension is the owner's to weigh at the gate, not the researcher's to resolve quietly.

## What it produces

A dated research note in the channel folder (`research-notes.md`), one per pass, newest last: the date, the pass, the findings in plain lines, and the source behind each finding — a search performed, a channel read, a document, a conversation recorded in the business's own files. The note is the evidence store; the backlog cites it. Each topic score's one-line reason names the finding it rests on ("search demand: high — the platform suggests this exact phrasing under the entry-point query, and two neighbour channels' strongest episodes answer it"), so anyone reading the backlog can walk any score back to its evidence. A score whose reason cites no finding is a judgment score, and it is marked as one until research replaces it.

## The re-rank and the refill

At the weekly read, the findings re-score the backlog: topics whose evidence strengthened rise, topics the market has moved past fall, and every change is written into the backlog with its reason — a re-rank nobody can trace to a finding is just judgment again, arriving by the back door. The weekly also checks the refill rule: when the unused topics in the backlog fall below what the channel's cadence needs for the next two weeks, a refill pass runs before the backlog runs dry — the four passes above, aimed at new candidates from the well — and the new candidates enter scored, with their evidence cited, in the same pass. A backlog discovered empty at stage 1 is a research failure that happened two weeks earlier; the refill rule exists so it is never discovered then.

## What the module is not

It is not trend-chasing. A topic riding a news cycle that the channel's well cannot support and its funnel cannot catch is recorded as observed and left alone; the channel's position outranks the week's attention. It is not a substitute for the channel's own numbers: once the scoreboard has real reads, counted results from the channel's own episodes outrank market research wherever the two disagree, the same way packaging's counted results outrank taste. And it is not a one-time setup chore. Markets move, platforms re-weight, buyers change their criteria; a research note older than the channel's review cycle is history, and scores resting on it are marked stale at the weekly read until the refresh replaces them.

## Verification items

- [ ] A research pass ran before the backlog was first scored, and the seed topics' score reasons cite its findings.

- [ ] Every topic score's reason cites a finding from a dated research note — or is marked as a judgment score, plainly, until research replaces it.

- [ ] The research note records all four passes: platform performance in the niche, search terms in the audience's phrasing, the other platforms' performing formats, and buyer demand signals with the channel's own deal flow read first.

- [ ] Every demand claim and every volume figure in the note carries its named source, and third-party estimates are recorded as estimates.

- [ ] The weekly read re-ranked the backlog against the refreshed findings, with each change's reason written into the backlog.

- [ ] The refill rule was checked at the weekly read: unused topics against the next two weeks of cadence, and a refill pass run in the same pass when the count fell short.

# Go direct — the doctrine

Source: Lulu Cheng Meservey, "GO DIRECT: THE MANIFESTO" (Flack, March 2024), paraphrased and credited; her talks add the two named principles at the end.

## The argument

Before the internet, reaching a large audience required the media, and reaching the media required professional publicists. That dependency is over: most of the planet is directly reachable, and the gatekeepers' own credibility has fallen. But comms habits have not changed — media still gets prioritized over the founder's own channels, and "corporate communications" produced by committee remains the default. A statement written by a dozen middle managers has one discernible purpose: to upset nobody.

Founders would never outsource their product. When it comes to convincing others to support the mission, the story is the product. Outsourcing comms is as bad as outsourcing code.

## Who speaks

The best spokesperson for an endeavor is not the most polished person or the one with the right credentials. It is the person who holds the knowledge upon which the enterprise is built — who can describe the idea and, against opposition, fight for it and win. Founders are irreplaceable as evangelists. Those who are stubborn, unorthodox, and disagreeable should not have their edges filed down to please entrenched interests.

Going direct is also a competitive edge precisely because it is uncomfortable: communications failures are public and personally humiliating, so most founders avoid the job. Doing it well pays in recruiting, fundraising, selling, and shaping the environment the company operates in.

## What "direct" does and does not mean

It means: crafting and telling your own story without being dependent on intermediaries.

It does not mean: doing every comms task yourself forever, refusing help or amplification, boycotting the press, or simply posting more. Dependence is the failure, not assistance.

## Two working principles from her talks

- **Resistance is escalation.** Answering a hostile narrative is itself a move; it raises the stakes and the attention. Choose it deliberately, never by reflex.
- **Ship-to-yap ratio.** Talk is priced by what has been shipped underneath it. A founder whose output is mostly statements about future products is spending credibility, not building it.

# Distribution and response

## The everyday engine (Vaynerchuk)

Source: Gary Vaynerchuk, Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook (2013) and his document-don't-create practice. Paraphrased from the frameworks; his corpus is mostly spoken, so treat these as disciplines, not texts.

- **Jabs before the hook.** Give value in each platform's native language, repeatedly, with no ask attached. The eventual ask (the right hook) lands in proportion to the jabs that preceded it.
- **Document, don't create.** The running record of the work — decisions, builds, failures, fixes — is the content supply. It does not run out, it is true by construction, and a competitor cannot copy it.
- **Day-trade attention.** Attention is underpriced somewhere at all times and the somewhere moves. Part of the everyday job is noticing where it is cheap now and showing up early, in that place's native format.
- **Volume is reps.** Consistency is the mechanism: the audience and the position are built by accumulation, not by one perfect post.

Governor on all of it: Meservey's ship-to-yap ratio. Distribution volume applies to documenting shipped work. The larger the gap between what is said and what has shipped, the more each post spends credibility instead of building it.

Scope note: in announcement mode this engine carries one message for days. In crisis mode it mostly switches off — a crisis is not content, and treating it as content is how a bad week becomes a character verdict.

## The plain-writing gate (Graham)

Source: Paul Graham, "Write Like You Talk" (2015) and "How to Write Usefully" (2020). Paraphrased.

- Write the way you talk. If a sentence would not survive being said aloud to a smart friend, rewrite it until it would.
- Useful writing is important, novel to the reader, and correct. A statement missing any of the three is filler, and filler in a founder's own channel teaches the audience to skip the founder.
- Stilted writing usually marks unclear thinking. Fix the thought, not the sentence.

## Response rules, expanded

- **Resistance is escalation** (Meservey). A rebuttal is a bid to raise the story's stakes and lengthen its life. Make it when the record is genuinely wrong and a correction will be carried by witnesses — not to win the afternoon.
- **Admissions are complete or not yet made.** If events are still arriving, the honest holding line is: here is what is true, here is what we are still establishing, here is when we will speak again. A staged admission that events overtake converts one story into two and spends the credibility the first one cost.
- **Confidence is debt.** "The board has full confidence" is a loan taken against future events. If there is any realistic path where the confident position is reversed within weeks, either do not borrow, or borrow small: describe the process ("an independent review is underway") instead of pre-judging its outcome.
- **One correction, complete, with documents.** A correction that dribbles out in rounds keeps the story on the front page and repositions you a rung lower each round.
- **Endings are stated from the record.** When a signed document ends a matter, quote its substance, link it, and stop. Let witnesses write the headline.

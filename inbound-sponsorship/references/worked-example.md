# Worked example: one inquiry, walked end to end

A public example of the procedure in [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md), written as shapes only. The publisher, the agency, and the brand are composites. Every figure is illustrative, chosen to show the shape of the math and the wording, not to quote any real property's prices.

## The setup

The publisher is a mid-size crypto media property: a news site, a daily newsletter, and a video channel. It sells sponsored articles and link insertions on a published card, and custom packages by proposal. Its standard placement term is 12 months. It already covers the betting category editorially, and no sponsor holds betting exclusivity.

## The inquiry

An email arrives from an account manager at a digital agency, on the agency's company domain. The agency represents a named international betting brand. The ask, in their words: publish an article about the brand on the publisher's site, with a permanent placement, a dofollow link, and guaranteed Google indexing. The closing line: "Could you please share your price and let us know if this is possible?"

No amount is named. No timeline is given.

## Step 1 — Triage

**Gate 1, category and brand safety:** the brand is a real, identifiable operator; the category is one the publisher already covers; no exclusivity conflict. Pass.

**Gate 2, inventory fit:** a sponsored article slot exists. Pass.

**Gate 3, compliance fit:** two of the three requested terms fail as written. The publisher does not sell permanent placements (its standard term is 12 months), and indexing is a platform outcome no publisher can guarantee. The dofollow request also runs against the property's link policy for paid placements. These are term failures, not buyer failures: the buyer is legitimate, so the move is to decline the terms and offer the compliant shape, not to disqualify the inquiry.

**Qualification pass:** Budget — not stated, and this is a custom shape, so no form bracket applies; the budget gets discovered in the reply (step 3). Authority — an agency account manager; the end client is named, which is the mark of a real brief; who approves spend on the client side is still unknown and worth one question later. Need — stated plainly: an article, a link, permanence, indexing. Timeline — not stated; the reply asks.

**Verdict: custom lane.** Two non-standard terms (permanent, guaranteed indexing) force the custom lane under step 2 regardless of deal size.

## Step 2 — Lane

Custom lane rules now govern: scope before numbers, no card quoted, no naked price given even though the buyer asked for one directly. The permanent term is priced from the buyer's budget at a high multiple of the 12-month rate — but first the buyer has to accept that 12 months is the standard and permanent is the exception, and name what the exception is worth to them.

## Step 3 — The draft reply

The reply does four jobs: confirms the placement is possible, resets the two failing terms in plain words, moves the budget question into a bracket anchored high first, and asks the one missing qualification fact (timeline). It quotes no card price, because a card price here would anchor a custom negotiation at card level.

> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for reaching out. Yes, we run paid article placements on [site] with a link to the brand.
>
> Two of your terms work differently here. Our standard placement runs 12 months, and we monitor it across the term. We do not keep a list price for permanent placements; those are custom arrangements, so they come down to budget. And our articles are indexed in the normal course, but indexing is Google's call, so we cannot guarantee it as a deal term.
>
> On budget: custom placements like this usually land between [high figure] and [higher figure] for a 12-month term, depending on scope, and permanent terms price above that range. If permanent is what the brand wants, tell me the budget you have for it and I will scope it properly.
>
> What timeline are you working to?
>
> Best,
> [Publisher] partnerships team

Why the bracket is built that way: the range is stated high number first, so the anchor the buyer reacts to is the publisher's, not a number the buyer invents cold (step 3, rule 5). The bracket is wide because scope is genuinely unknown — a single article and a permanent term are different products. And the question is not "what is your budget?" but "tell me the budget you have for *this*" — the money attached to a named outcome, which buyers answer far more readily.

## What happens next, on each branch

**The buyer names a budget inside or above the bracket.** Scope D.U.E. (one article or a series? any reuse of the content as paid media? category exclusivity, and for how long?), then answer with two or three packages of equal value to the publisher that differ across term, scope, and deliverables — a 12-month package, a longer-term package, a package with newsletter inclusion — and let the pick reveal the real budget (step 3, rule 3).

**The buyer names a budget below the bracket.** The inquiry drops to the card lane's floor test: offer the standard 12-month card placement at the published price, or decline into a lane (step 1, guardrail 7). The permanent term is not discounted into existence.

**The buyer insists on the indexing guarantee or the permanent term at a card price.** Both are pre-authorized disqualifiers once the compliant version has been offered and refused. Decline politely, restate the standard shape once, and close. No third round.

**The buyer goes quiet.** One follow-up at the dated next step set in the reply thread, then the inquiry is parked. Silence after a clear offer is itself a qualification answer.

## The contrast: the same week, a card-lane inquiry

A second email lands the same week: a named software company, writing from its own domain, asks the price of a link insertion in an existing article. Standard inventory, standard term, no special asks. Triage takes one pass, the lane is card, and the reply quotes the published insertion price, attaches the media kit, and asks for the target URL, the anchor text, and the timing in the same message. One reply, no bracket, no scoping call — because in this game both sides already know the zone, and speed is worth more than discovery.

# Hybrid clause templates

Fill-in clause language for the deal structures in [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) step 4 and the lifecycle in step 5. Bracketed fields are filled from the property's own terms. This is working language for a draft the approver reviews, not legal advice; contracts above the property's own review threshold go to counsel.

Each template is short on purpose. A clause the buyer cannot follow on first reading is a clause that gets negotiated instead of signed.

## 1. The floor clause (hybrid deals)

> The fee for this placement is [flat fee] for the [term], plus [commission rate] on tracked sales above [threshold, if any]. The flat fee is guaranteed and is not offset against commission. Commission is upside, not a substitute for the fee.

Why it is written this way: the flat fee is the publisher's floor. If commission could offset it, the deal would be pure CPA wearing a hybrid label.

## 2. The guarantee boundary clause

> We guarantee [impressions / traffic / placement position] as described in the rate card for the term. We do not guarantee conversions, sales, search rankings, or indexing, which depend on your offer, your pages, and third-party platforms outside our control.

Why: the publisher guarantees what it controls. This sentence, signed before the campaign, is what ends the argument after a campaign underperforms.

## 3. The test box clause

> This engagement begins as a [30]-day test covering [inventory]. The success measure is [number and metric]. At the end of the test we either expand on the terms in this agreement or the engagement ends, with no obligation on either side beyond the test fee of [amount].

Why: a boxed test converts a pure-CPA ask into a paid experiment with a decision date. An open-ended "trial" with no measure and no date is a free option for the buyer.

## 4. The tracking clause

> All placements carry a unique tracking link [and/or code: details]. Performance is reported from [tracking source] every [period]. Tracking applies to flat-fee placements as well, so both sides hold the same numbers at renewal.

Why: tracking on the first deal is what prices the second. A flat-fee deal without tracking renews blind.

## 5. The term, monitoring, and remedy clause (placements)

> This placement runs for [12 months] from the publication date. We monitor it during the term. If the placement comes down or the link breaks during the term, we will restore it, replace it with an equal-tier placement, or credit the unused portion, at our choice. At term end the placement renews under clause 6 or the link is removed.

Why: the term stated in writing is what stops "permanent" from being the buyer's default assumption. The remedy menu (restore, replace, credit) matches market practice in the placement segment.

## 6. The renewal and auto-charge clause

> This placement renews for successive [12-month] terms at the then-current rate card price unless either side declines in writing at least [30] days before the term ends. Payment is collected automatically on the payment method on file at the start of each term. We will notify you of any price change at least [30] days before it takes effect.

Why: billing set to charge itself at signature turns renewal into a scope conversation instead of a collections chase. The notice periods keep the automation honest; a surprise charge is a chargeback and a lost sponsor.

## 7. The disclosure clause

> Paid placements are labeled as sponsored content, clearly and conspicuously, and paid links carry rel="sponsored". This labeling is a legal requirement of our publishing and is not a negotiable term.

Why: naming disclosure as law, in the agreement, ends the "can we skip the label this once?" negotiation before it starts. See guardrail 4 in the main skill.

## 8. The pure-CPA counter (reply language, not a contract clause)

> We do not run performance-only placements. What we can do is a guaranteed flat fee of [amount] for [inventory and term], with [commission rate] on tracked sales on top, starting as a [30]-day test. That structure is how every performance deal on this property runs.

Why: it answers the brand-side sequence (pure CPA first, hybrid only under refusal) with the floor and the test box in one move, and it states the shape as house policy rather than as a reaction to this buyer.

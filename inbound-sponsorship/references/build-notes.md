# Build notes — the four-skill pass on version 0.1.0

Before this skill was called done, its draft was run through four method skills from the same library: first-principles-thinking, game-theory-for-decisions, fundraising, and comms. Each pass below names what it checked and what it changed. A pass that changes nothing is recorded as changing nothing; none of the four came back empty.

## 1. First-principles-thinking — decompose the claims

**The decision under review:** which rules may this skill state as fact?

Every load-bearing claim was listed with its source and its evidence type, then stripped to what the source actually supports. The assumptions table that mattered:

| # | Assumption in the first draft | Source | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | "Publish your prices" (or its opposite) as a single rule | Media-kit guides vs. creator doctrine | Both absolutes fail. The surviving truth is conditional: the right move depends on who knows the bargaining zone. Rebuilt as the two-lane policy. |
| A2 | "78% of buyers buy from the first responder" | Vendor folklore | No primary source exists. Cut as a claim; kept only as a named myth the skill forbids (step 6). |
| A3 | "Three tiers lift the average ticket 15–30%" | Vendor content, no methodology | Cut as an effect size. The MESO experiments support packages on outcome and cooperation, not on a ticket percentage. The skill teaches the supported claim only. |
| A4 | "Set the hybrid floor at 50–70% of the flat rate" | Practitioner convention (Dealvio, AVID) | Kept, labeled `practitioner`. It is a convention a property can adopt, not a finding. |
| A5 | "The lane boundary is $15,000–$25,000" | B2B SaaS transparency writing; the BANT-to-MEDDIC switch point | Kept, labeled `borrowed` and "rough" in both places it appears, with a note that a property's own data should replace it. |
| A6 | "Sponsors decide within 90 seconds of opening a kit" | One podcast guide, unverified | Cut entirely. It carried no rule, only decoration. |
| A7 | "Response speed wins deals" | Oldroyd 2007; HBR 2011 | Narrowed to what was measured: contact and qualification, not closed revenue. The limit is stated in step 6 and in the evidence notes. |

**What the pass changed:** the evidence-label system itself (`studied` / `borrowed` / `practitioner`) became guardrail 3 and a label on every rule; two claims were cut outright (A2 as a claim, A6 entirely); three claims were demoted to their true label (A3, A4, A5); one claim was narrowed (A7).

## 2. Game-theory-for-decisions — the lane policy as a game

**Players:** the publisher (seller of dated, perishable inventory); the buyer (a brand, or an agency acting for one — often a professional, repeat bargainer); sometimes an intermediary platform taking a cut of the sequence.

**Information:** in the card lane the bargaining zone is public knowledge — both sides can price the inventory, so the seller moving first captures the anchor and a posted price protects the seller against the stronger bargainer. In the custom lane the buyer holds the private information (budget, campaign value, internal approval), so a seller who names a naked number first anchors against themselves (Maaravi & Levy 2017).

**Sequence:** scoping D.U.E. before numbers is an information move, not a courtesy: it changes what both sides know before the first figure exists. The MESO step changes the game again — instead of asking the buyer to reveal a budget (which a rational buyer shades low), the seller offers packages and lets the buyer's *choice* reveal it.

**Repeated play:** sponsors and agencies recur across properties and across years. The impasse and satisfaction costs in the meta-analysis (g = −0.42, g = −0.40) are therefore priced into rule 2 of step 3: an aggressive anchor that wins once and ends the relationship is a losing move in the repeated game.

**What the pass changed:** the lane test was rewritten from a product test ("is it standardized?") to an information test ("who knows the bargaining zone?"), with standardization as the usual sign; the counterparty-sequence note was added to step 4 (brand-side playbooks push pure CPA first and concede hybrid only under refusal — the seller should recognize the sequence and counter once, with floor plus test box); guardrail 6 (protect the floor) was added, because a draft that reveals the reservation price destroys the seller's position in every later round.

## 3. Fundraising — qualify fast, always be closing

The fundraising skill's shared core was applied to the desk steps, in its own terms:

- **Qualify fast.** Qualifying is closing: every question must earn the next minute. Applied: step 1's qualification pass is four questions, asked once, on the first reply — and the disqualifiers are pre-authorized so qualification never waits for a meeting. The fundraising guardrail that numbers need an approver became guardrail 1 (drafts before send).

- **Surface money and capacity early, casually.** Applied, with the negotiation evidence as the constraint on *how*: money surfaces early as a bracket or a package pick, never as a naked "what's your budget?" — the fundraising instinct and the anchoring evidence agree on the goal and the bracket is the mechanism that satisfies both.

- **Always be closing.** No exchange ends without an interest band, an indicative size, and a dated next step with an owner; silence on size is failure; "circle back" is rejected. Applied verbatim as the closing set at the end of step 3 and as item 5 of the output contract.

- **Qualify out cleanly and keep the door open.** Fundraising passes cleanly and asks "who else should we meet?" Applied as guardrail 7: disqualify into a lane (card, lower tier, affiliate program, backfill), fast and politely, so today's wrong-fit inquiry is next quarter's right-fit sponsor.

**What the pass changed:** the closing set was added to step 3; the output contract gained its dated-next-step item in its current strict form; guardrail 1 was worded to name numbers and terms explicitly as approver-gated.

## 4. Comms — the output contract and the draft shapes

The comms skill's plain-writing gate and output discipline were applied to everything a buyer or an approver reads:

- **The output is a fixed return set, in a fixed order.** Comms returns the same four parts on every run; this skill returns five (verdict, what they said, the money, the draft reply, the next step), verdict first, so the approver reads the decision before the evidence.

- **Say it plainly.** Short sentences, one idea each, figures spoken the way a person says them. Applied to the draft templates: the worked-example reply and the clause templates were rewritten until each sentence survives being read aloud. The pure-CPA counter in the clause file is four sentences.

- **One ask per message.** A draft reply that asks for budget, timeline, assets, and approval in one breath gets none of them. The worked-example reply asks for exactly one thing after its term resets: the timeline. The bracket carries the budget question inside the statement of the range, so it does not count as a second ask.

- **Correctness is a gate.** The final text of every template passed a grammar read as its own step, separate from the strategy read.

**What the pass changed:** the output contract took its current five-part shape; two templates (the floor clause and the CPA counter) were shortened by roughly a third; the worked-example draft lost a second question it originally carried.

## Standing result

The package that ships as 0.1.0 is the draft after all four passes. The labels, the lane test, the closing set, and the output contract are the visible residue of this record.

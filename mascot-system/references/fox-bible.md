# The character bible — the Made Man fox system

This is the filled bible for the Made Man fox series. It mirrors the locked art direction in `~/workspace/ian-balina-website/DESIGN.md` ("Locked art direction," Ian, 2026-11-24). DESIGN.md is the governing source: if this bible and DESIGN.md ever disagree, DESIGN.md wins and this file is updated to match it.

## Part 1 — Series rules

**Series name:** the Made Man fox system.

**The one-line look:** a lean, adult fox with deadpan dot eyes directs fleets of sleek matte robots at work, drawn in Peanuts-style minimal ink with flat limited color on cream, always text-free.

**Cast rule:** animals and robots only. Never humans. The fox is the lead in every piece. Robots are the workforce. Counterparties, when a piece needs one, are cast as animals at natural proportions — the bear is the standing counterparty (the buyer, the other side). No being appears without an entry in Part 2.

**Locked style block:** "Peanuts-style minimal ink cartoon, simple confident black ink lines, flat limited colors, sparse background, drawn for adults. The fox is lean and adult: tall upright proportions, a long pointed snout, one small plain black dot eye, a calm deadpan expression — never round, big-eyed, stocky, or childish. Robots are sleek minimal machines with matte shells and plain dot eyes, no mouths, no smiles, no cute proportions. The art is strictly text-free: no letters, numbers, words, logos, or signage anywhere."

**Panel frame spec:** one set of numbers per piece, set by the piece's own compose step; the standing carousel spec is a wide landscape panel, thin black border, cream page #FAF6EC, slide canvas 1080x1020.

**Palette:** paper cream #FAF6EC; ink #141414; the fox's warm orange coat and cream markings and the robots' matte gray are recorded in their entries. Background tones stay sparse and in palette.

**Line style:** minimal ink, confident single-weight lines, economy of detail; a panel that turns glossy, busy, or heavily rendered has left the register.

**Text-layer defaults for the series:** headline, caption, and signature layers are set as type at compose time, never drawn into the art. Deck and carousel pieces run with all three layers on.

**Signature block contents:** the brand's own handles — X, LinkedIn, YouTube — plus the page number, in that order, set once per piece template, never retyped per panel.

## Part 2 — Character entries

### The Made Man fox

**Name:** the Made Man fox.

**Species and role:** a fox; the lead. He is Ian — the founder, the decision-maker, the one directing the work. He appears in every piece.

**Proportions:** lean, tall, and adult. He stands upright on long slim legs; his head is carried high on a long neck line; the snout is long and pointed, drawn in profile or three-quarter view. His tail is large and carried low behind him. He is never round, stocky, short-legged, or chibi, and never drawn with a big round head.

**The eyes:** one small plain black dot eye (an oval dot in profile), no white of the eye, no highlight, no pupil ring. A single short curved brow line above the eye. The expression is calm and deadpan: the mouth is a short straight or nearly straight line. Expression changes live in the brow and the mouth line only — never in a new eye style, never a grin, never raised cartoon eyebrows.

**Palette:** warm orange coat; cream muzzle sweeping down the throat into a full cream chest and belly; cream inner ears with dark ear tips; black nose; large cream tail tip; ink #141414 on cream #FAF6EC.

**Line style notes:** minimal ink, flat limited color, no rendering or shading beyond a flat tone. The cream chest sweep — muzzle to belly in one shape — is part of his silhouette and must survive every pose.

**Signature props and wardrobe:** none. He wears no clothes and carries no standing prop; props belong to the scene (a lever, a book, a card), never to the character.

**Reference set:** the standing anchors listed in Part 3. Every generation of the fox carries style-a-carousel.jpg plus the deck scene closest to the panel's setting.

**Drift notes:** the recurring failure is a rounder, stockier, big-eyed fox — a children's-cartoon take that reads as a different character. It came from anchoring to superseded takes (the game-theory strip fox and the agents-carousel fox are retired and must never be used as anchors). The fix that holds: anchor to the Option A deck scenes named in Part 3 and name the traits in the prompt — "lean and adult, tall proportions, long pointed snout, one small plain black dot eye, calm deadpan expression, never round or stocky." A second scar: the fox arriving with a grin or visible teeth. The deadpan line is "mouth is a short straight line, no grin, no teeth."

### The robots

**Name:** the robots (the agent fleet).

**Species and role:** sleek machines; the workforce. They do the work the fox directs — typing, filming, editing, carrying, shelving. They never lead a piece and never upstage the fox.

**Proportions:** adult machine proportions, never cute: a smooth elongated oval head on a slim articulated body, seated at a desk or standing on a pedestal base. No round chibi bodies, no stubby limbs.

**The eyes:** two small plain black oval dot eyes. No highlights, no pupils. There is no mouth: no smile, no grin, no mouth line of any kind.

**Palette:** matte light-gray shells with slightly darker gray joints; a plain round ear disc on the side of the head; ink #141414 outlines on cream #FAF6EC.

**Line style notes:** minimal ink, flat matte tones, a few panel seams and joint circles at most. No chrome shine, no glowing parts (a tiny indicator dot is the most that is allowed, and only where an anchor already has one).

**Signature props and wardrobe:** none of their own; their props are their tools — keyboards, cameras, film reels, books — supplied by the scene.

**Reference set:** the same standing anchors as the fox; the robots are drawn from the regenerated deck scenes (art-no-employees.jpg and art-playbook.jpg show them best), never from the retired round white robots in the original style-options sheet.

**Drift notes:** the recurring failures are a smile creeping onto the face and the body shrinking into a round cute mascot. Both fail the panel outright. The wording that holds: "sleek matte gray robot, smooth elongated oval head, two plain black dot eyes, no mouth at all, no smile, slim adult machine proportions."

### The bear (standing counterparty)

**Name:** the bear.

**Species and role:** a brown bear; the standing counterparty — the buyer, the other side of the deal. Cast in this role in every piece that needs one.

**Proportions:** a large natural bear, upright, bigger than the fox, at natural animal proportions.

**The eyes:** small plain dark dot eyes, calm expression.

**Palette:** solid brown coat, lighter muzzle, black nose.

**Reference set:** art-invitation.jpg (with the fox and a robot at the book table).

**Drift notes:** none recorded yet.

**Retired anchors — never use:** the game-theory strip fox (`~/workspace/typefully/previews/carousel-game-theory/strip.png`) and the agents-carousel fox are superseded takes. Any panel anchored to them is anchored to the wrong character.

**Working anchors:** within one piece, every approved panel joins the working set at once, and later panels are generated against the standing anchors plus the working set — at minimum the immediately prior approved panel, plus any earlier panel sharing the setting, the props, or the secondary characters.

**Promotion:** an approved panel that shows the fox cleanly — full face, standard build, nothing occluding — may be promoted into the standing set at the end of a run, recorded here. A panel that passed narrowly is not promoted.

**Currency:** the chain only points at approved, current images. When a panel is regenerated, its old version leaves every set it was in, and anything generated against it is re-checked.

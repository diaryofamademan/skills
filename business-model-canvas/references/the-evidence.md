# The evidence

Every load-bearing claim in `SKILL.md`, with its source, its standing, and its limits. Grades: **(a)** measured research, peer-reviewed; **(b)** practitioner framework — the field's standard instrument, used for the discipline of its questions, never as causal evidence.

## The frames

**The nine-block canvas — grade (b), canonical practitioner frame.** Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation* (2010), from Osterwalder's doctoral research and a large practitioner community. Nine blocks — customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure — and, in the book itself, a five-phase process (mobilize, understand, design, implement, manage) and an environment analysis covering market forces, industry forces, key trends, and macroeconomic forces. The popular one-page sheet drops the process and the environment; this skill restores the environment as Step 3 because the criticisms below are correct that the sheet alone omits it.

**The Value Proposition Canvas — grade (b).** Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda, and Smith, *Value Proposition Design* (2014). A zoom on two of the nine blocks: the customer profile (jobs, pains, gains) against the value map (products and services, pain relievers, gain creators), with fit as the explicit question. Used here as Step 2's module for the lead segment.

**The Lean Canvas — grade (b).** Ash Maurya, *Running Lean* (2012) and the Lean Canvas tool. An adaptation of the nine blocks for the pre-fit state: problem, existing alternatives, solution, key metrics, and unfair advantage replace or sharpen the blocks a searching business cannot yet honestly fill (partners, activities, relationships at scale). Its premise — that a searching business's plan is a set of untested guesses to be run as experiments — is the premise of this skill's Step 4.

## The one direct test

**Ladd (2018) — grade (a), and sobering.** Timothy Ladd, "Does the Business Model Canvas Drive Venture Success?", *Journal of Research in Marketing and Entrepreneurship*. In a sample of 271 accelerator teams, business model canvas use was associated with placing in pitch competitions — and not with profit. Of the individual blocks, only customer segmentation showed a clear correlation with outcomes; the study raises the possibility that the canvas contains unhelpful components. **What the skill takes from it:** the canvas cannot be sold as a performance intervention. Its defensible use is narrower — a shared record of the model and a device for surfacing assumptions — and the skill's weight sits on the assumptions and their tests, not on the polish of the sheet. A skill that hid this study would be selling the sheet; this one builds around it.

## The criticisms, at full strength

**Coes (2014) and the critical literature.** The standing charges against the canvas: it is a **static snapshot** of a business that moves; it **ignores competition and environment** — no block asks who else is playing; it offers **no prioritization** — nine blocks of equal visual weight, as if a cost line and a value proposition matter equally; and it is **silent on evolution** — nothing in the sheet says how a model should change or when the sheet itself expires. Later critics add that the blocks' apparent completeness can substitute for thought: a full canvas feels like an analyzed business. **The skill's answers, and their limits:** the environment pass (Step 3) addresses competition and context, partially — a page of forces is not a competitive strategy. The ranked assumptions list (Step 4) supplies prioritization, of risks rather than of blocks. Dated change triggers (Step 5) address stasis, as a discipline rather than a property of the sheet. The criticisms remain in this file, standing, because the answers reduce them without repealing them.

## What is not claimed anywhere in this skill

No source in this file shows that filling in a canvas causes a business to succeed, that any block beyond segmentation has measured outcome weight, or that one sheet outperforms another in a controlled comparison — the fit test's preference for the Lean sheet while searching is a practitioner's argument (Maurya's), adopted for the honesty of its questions, and labeled as such in the procedure.

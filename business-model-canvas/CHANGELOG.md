# Changelog

## 0.1.3 — 2026-11-21

Sibling calls wired, under the playbook rule: a skill calls the sibling skills its work needs, by name, at the step that needs them. The four-skill pass shaped this skill at build time, but the procedure itself named no calls — a run could complete without ever taking them. Now they are steps:

- **Step 0 calls first-principles-thinking** — the fit test reads the business's state off a decomposition to measured terms, not off the business's self-description.

- **Step 3 calls game-theory-for-decisions** — the environment pass's partner and channel reading is run as a game, and the incentive map it returns is what the pass records.

- **Step 4 calls fix-the-bottleneck** — the top-ranked assumption is treated as the system's constraint, and its kill test is sequenced first.

## 0.1.2 — 2026-11-21

Icons on the rendered canvas. The field test's grid shipped clean but bare; the Strategyzer sheet it is modeled on carries an icon in every block header, and the house standard for visual work is the Hugeicons free library.

- **The output contract names the icon library.** Every block header on the rendered canvas carries a Hugeicons (free version) icon chosen for what the block means — stroke style, one consistent weight. Icons sit on structural elements only (block headers, section markers); an icon that carries no meaning is decoration and does not ship. The field test's canvas was regenerated under the rule: an alert mark on Problem, a bulb on Solution, a diamond on the value proposition, a shield on Unfair Advantage, a route on Channels, a money bag on Revenue Streams, and the rest to match.

## 0.1.1 — 2026-11-21

Field-test format fix, from field test 1 (a real company's canvas, filed the same day). The first filed record shipped in analyst prose — long, dense verdict sections — even though the output contract already carried a plain-style writing pass. The pass governed the canvas record but never named the field-test record, so the human-read parts of the filed test escaped it.

- **Field-test records now open plain.** A filed field-test record opens with a plain section — the verdict and the headline findings in short sentences a person would say out loud — before any working detail.

- **Verdict sections are plain in their own right.** The fit verdict and the verdict on the skill must be written plain, not summarized plainly over dense prose. The output contract names the human-read parts of a field-test record — the opening and every verdict — as plain-style territory; working detail below the opening may stay as dense as the evidence needs. A checklist item carries the requirement into the pre-delivery pass.

- **The rendered canvas is now a required deliverable of every run.** The same field test made the second gap plain: the run returned a text record, and the reader expected the canvas itself — the filled grid, in the Strategyzer layout. The output contract now requires it: the chosen sheet drawn as a grid visual, delivered as a PDF and an image alongside the record, entries shortened only to fit, evidence and assumption labels kept, the fit verdict on the sheet.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-21

First build. One skill, two sheets: a fit test decides whether the business is still searching for its model (the Lean Canvas) or executing one it has found (the nine-block Business Model Canvas), the chosen sheet is filled from evidence with every guess labeled, and the run finishes where the sheet's value actually is — a ranked list of the riskiest assumptions, each with the cheapest test that could kill it.

- **The name is the category term.** Business Model Canvas, not Lean Canvas: the skill covers both sheets, and naming it for Maurya's variant would borrow his brand to describe half the skill. The fit test, not the title, is where the Lean sheet lives.

- **The fit test is Step 0 and its verdict is a required output field.** Three questions, in order — is the problem proven, is the solution bought repeatedly, are the economics demonstrated — with a mixed-state rule (decide by the weaker half; the model, not the product, is the unit). A searching business on the nine-block sheet invents partners and programs; a found business on the Lean sheet hides from its scale questions. The test exists so neither happens by preference.

- **The criticisms are answered inside the procedure, and kept standing in the limits.** Coes (2014) and others: static snapshot, no competition or environment, no prioritization between blocks. The skill adds an environment pass (with partners and channels read as incentive problems), a ranked assumptions list as the prioritization, and dated change triggers with a review date. The limits section says plainly that these answers are partial.

- **Ladd 2018 is carried in the open.** The one direct test — 271 accelerator teams; pitch placing moved, profit did not; only customer segmentation clearly correlated; the canvas may contain unhelpful components — sits in the evidence reference and the limits, and shapes the design: the canvas is claimed as a recording and stress-testing instrument, never as a performance intervention, and the method's weight sits on segmentation precision and the assumptions, where the evidence and the logic agree it belongs.

- **The worked example is de-identified and composite.** An invented scheduling tool for independent dental clinics, still searching: the fit test refuses the flattering sheet, the unfair-advantage block is recorded empty rather than filled, and the top assumption's test is a letter with a price on it. Shapes only, per the house rule for examples drawn from practice.

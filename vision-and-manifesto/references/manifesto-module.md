# The manifesto module

The manifesto is the core at public length: the beliefs written out in full, for strangers. It is the only artifact in this skill that is gated on a *reason to exist*, because public belief language is not free. It spends the company's credibility, it attracts and repels real people, and — unlike the vision and mission, which coordinate insiders — it cannot be quietly revised when it turns out wrong. So the module fires only under a named public job, and the job is written into the record before a word is drafted.

## The three named jobs

- **Recruiting self-selection.** The company hires often enough, and the beliefs are demanding enough, that filtering before the interview saves everyone time. A working manifesto makes the wrong candidates leave and the right ones lean in; both directions are the job. If hiring is rare or the beliefs ask nothing of anyone, this job is not real.

- **Movement-building.** The company is asking people who will never buy anything to join, spread, or defend something — an open standard, a cause, a way of working. The manifesto is the joining document: it must say what members believe, what they do, and what joining costs.

- **Belief-as-product.** The belief is itself part of what is sold — customers buy partly *because* the company holds it, and would leave if it stopped. The manifesto is then a product document, held to a product's standard: it must be true in the way a spec sheet is true.

A job that fits none of the three — "we should have a manifesto, competitors do," "the founder likes writing them" — does not open the module. The record states the module was skipped and why; that line is a deliverable, not an omission.

## The entry test

Before drafting, the record answers three questions:

1. **Who is this for, by name of role?** Not "the public": the applicant deciding whether to apply, the practitioner deciding whether to join, the customer deciding whether the belief is worth paying for.

2. **What should the reader do differently after reading?** Apply, not apply, join, repeat the argument to someone else. A manifesto with no intended act is an essay; essays are comms' work, at comms' standards.

3. **What will it cost us if it works?** A manifesto that filters will shrink an applicant pool; one that declares beliefs will be quoted back at the company on its worst day. The record states the accepted cost before the text exists, so the cost cannot be renegotiated at the first discomfort.

## Structure

Written from the same core as the vision and mission — every section traceable to a belief in the record:

1. **The scene, in full.** The future from Step 1, uncompressed. This is the one place the scene ships as written.

2. **The beliefs, in order.** Each stated as a behavior, with the costly decision from the excavation that proves the company holds it. Proof is what separates a manifesto from a mood.

3. **The refusals.** What the company will not do, become, or sell — the anti-beliefs, at the same length and specificity as the beliefs. A manifesto without refusals is advertising.

4. **The invitation.** The act the named job requires, asked for plainly: apply, join, build with us, hold us to this. One invitation, matched to the job — a manifesto that asks for everything asks for nothing.

## What a manifesto must never do

- **Say more than the core.** Any claim in the manifesto that is not in the vision record is cut or the core is reopened — never the reverse. The public text is the most compressed evidence standard, not the loosest.

- **Promise what the gate has not passed.** If the decision test named three decisions, the manifesto may cite those three. It may not imply a company-wide transformation the gate never verified.

- **Attack by name.** Refusals state what *we* will not do. Naming competitors or enemies turns belief into positioning, badly, and dates the document to a fight the company will outgrow.

- **Hide its date.** A manifesto is a belief at a moment. It carries its date, and revisions are new editions, not silent edits — the same rule as any public record the company will be held to.

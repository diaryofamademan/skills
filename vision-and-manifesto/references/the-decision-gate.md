# The decision gate

The gate is the skill's definition of done. A vision that changes no decision is wall text with better research behind it, and this file exists so the test cannot be waved through. It runs after the vision and mission are drafted (Step 4), and again at every review where the decision log has gone quiet.

## Why a gate at all

The evidence is consistent on one point: statements do not act; decisions do. Bart, Bontis and Taggar (2001) found mission statements related to performance only through changed behavior and alignment. Gartenberg, Prat and Serafeim (2019) found purpose unrelated to performance until it was paired with clarity — people below the executive floor able to say what the purpose meant for their own work. The gate therefore tests the two things the evidence says carry the effect: **decisions changed** and **clarity mapped**. Everything else in the skill is writing; this is the audit.

## The decision test

Name at least three real decisions the statements change, spanning the three domains where beliefs become expensive:

- **Hiring.** Who gets in, and who gets promoted. A passing entry names a candidate profile the old default would have hired and the statement now refuses — or the reverse — and the trait in the core that decides it.

- **Firing.** What conduct, drift, or standard ends employment. A passing entry names the behavior the statement makes untenable that was previously tolerated, and confirms leadership would actually act on it.

- **Money.** What gets funded, priced, or refused. A passing entry names a spend the statement authorizes that the old default would have cut, or a revenue source it refuses that the old default would have taken.

Each entry states both sides: the old default, and what the statement now requires. An entry that cannot state the old default is describing a decision the company never faced, and it does not count.

**Cost is the quality bar.** Three cheap decisions pass the letter of the test and prove nothing. The reviewer weighs what each named decision costs — money forgone, a strong candidate refused, a comfortable person let go. A gate passed entirely on costless decisions is recorded as a pass in form and a failure in substance, and the record says so.

**Enforcement is part of the entry.** A decision leadership would not enforce under pressure is a wish. For each entry, the record names who enforces it when it becomes inconvenient — because it will, and the first inconvenience is the gate's real test.

## The clarity mapping test

Choose three people in three different roles, at least one of them far from the executive floor. Ask each, unprompted and separately: *what does the vision mean for your work this quarter?* Pass conditions:

- Each answer names work the person actually controls.

- The three answers map to the same future — different tasks, one destination.

- No answer is a recitation of the statement's words with no work attached.

Failure is a language problem before it is a people problem: the statement is simplified — shorter, more concrete, closer to the scene — and the test runs again. Purpose that cannot survive this mapping is, on the evidence, purpose that is not yet doing anything.

## Failure modes the gate catches

- **The poster.** Beautiful language, zero decisions. The commonest failure, and the one the meta-analyses measure.

- **The laundry list.** Nine values, every one sincerely held, none of them ever deciding anything because together they point everywhere. The four-value limit exists for this.

- **The borrowed future.** A vision copied from a admired company in a different game. It fails the excavation: no costly decision in this company's history supports it.

- **The two-text company.** One vision for the deck, one for the building. Guardrail 4 violation; the gate treats the existence of variants as automatic failure, whichever text is better.

- **The unfalsifiable objective.** "Be the leading platform for…" — no number, no date, no stranger-verifiable state. Fails Step 2 before it reaches the gate, and is recorded as a Step 2 failure so the pattern is visible in the record.

## The decision log

The gate is not a one-time ceremony. The record opens a decision log: every later decision the statements change, dated, with the statement cited. The log is reviewed on the record's review date. Two consecutive review cycles with an empty log trigger a full re-run from Step 0 — either the beliefs were never the company's, or the company has drifted from them, and the new record states which.

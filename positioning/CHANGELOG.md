# Changelog

## 0.2.1 — 2026-11-21

Sibling calls wired, under the playbook rule: a skill calls the sibling skills its work needs, by name, at the step that needs them. The four-skill pass shaped this skill at build time, but the procedure itself named no calls — a run could complete without ever taking them. Now they are steps: first-principles at the evidence step, comms at the statement step, one-page-marketing-plan as the handoff.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-21

The diagnostic half of Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* joins the skill. Schwartz was checked for across the marketing skills and was in none of them; what enters here is his diagnosis — two stages, named before a position is stated — and nothing else of the book.

- **Two stages, diagnosed at Step 4.** The best-fit customer's awareness stage (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware), read from what buyers say unprompted; and the market's sophistication stage, one to five, counted in the claims the Step 1 alternatives are already making. Both are recorded in the positioning record's supporting record as two new fields, and the sophistication field states how the owned concept therefore leads.

- **The lead-form rule.** The owned concept's lead form must match the two stages, and the record says how. At sophistication stages four and five, the concept leads with the checkable mechanism, never a bigger claim — when every rival claims the better outcome, an enlarged claim carries no weight, and the lead a buyer can still weigh is the mechanism working in the open. A field note from a run of this skill is carried in Step 4, de-identified: in a results-saturated market the concept that landed led with checkability itself, the published record as the mechanism, while a results-superlative claim failed in the same run.

- **The boundary is the point.** Schwartz's intensification craft — enlarged promises, dramatization, promise stacking — stays out, by name. It conflicts with the skill's claims discipline (no proof, no position) and does not meet the evidence standard the rest of the procedure runs on. Schwartz is labeled the way Ries and Trout are labeled in the framing axiom: a practitioner frame, not measured law, overruled by buying evidence wherever the two disagree. His mass-desire point — desire is channeled, never created — is cited as framing at Step 7's entry points, where it supports the existing discipline; it is not a step.

- **The chain-out block is unchanged.** The five fields the one-page-marketing-plan skill consumes verbatim stay five; the two stage fields live in the supporting record, and the plan's media square reads the awareness stage from the record. The worked example's Step 4 now works the two stages on its composite case, so the example matches the procedure it teaches.

## 0.1.1 — 2026-11-19

The output contract now calls in the writing pass, replacing the one-paragraph write-it-like-a-person rule. When a positioning record ships in raw strategy register, the owned concept reads as internal jargon and the record stops sounding like the buyers it is meant to win. The pass runs the work-humanizer skill in rewrite mode over the positioning record, then applies the writing-styles skill's plain rules, and the record is not done until the pass has run on the exact text being delivered. Facts, figures, quotes, names, dates, and statuses never change in the pass.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-18

First build. Positioning was built as its own skill rather than a section inside a marketing plan or a messaging guide, because the decision it runs — what position a company owns — is upstream of both: the one-page-marketing-plan skill's message square consumes this skill's record, comms says the position in public, and fundraising borrows it for a raise. A decision three other skills depend on gets its own procedure, its own gate, and its own output contract.

- **The procedure is Dunford's five components, in her order.** Competitive alternatives (from buyer evidence, the status quo named first when it is the largest), unique attributes (facts about the product, checked against the frame), value with proof (every claim paired with checkable proof or logged as unproved), best-fit customers (situational characteristics read backwards from the customers who already love it, one beachhead cut first), and market category last (an existing frame by default; a new category only on evidence that every existing frame misleads, with the teaching cost stated). Trend is the optional sixth, tested by subtraction: remove it and the position must still stand.

- **The objective is set by the buying evidence, not by folklore.** The Ehrenberg-Bass research program — measured, replicated buying-behavior patterns — sets the goal: to be thought of, by more category buyers, in more buying situations, with proof attached. Perceived differentiation alone is weak in that data, so difference is scoped by buying type: it is the proved argument for considered purchases, where win/loss evidence shows it deciding deals, and it is not a substitute for memory and availability in repeat ones. The counter-data (perceived difference as the largest factor in predisposition) is carried in the references with the resolution: the camps measure different things, and difference counts in this skill as an association measured in buyers, not as a claim.

- **The memory layer is Romaniuk's.** Category entry points — the buying situations, mapped across the 7 Ws, that a position must attach to — decide what the position is retrieved at; the Fame × Uniqueness audit decides whether the brand's distinctive assets can carry it, and sorts assets into carry, build, and retire. Measures are chosen at the close of the run: retrieval at the named entry points, concept association versus competitors, asset movement, beachhead penetration. A position that cannot state how its landing would be measured is sent back by the gate as unfinished.

- **Segment discipline and the output check are Moore's.** One beachhead segment first, dominated fully, with adjacent segments as later moves; the whole-product test at the gate (can the beachhead actually reach the promised outcome with what exists today, gaps recorded as launch conditions); and the positioning statement template used as the record's completeness check — an unfillable slot names the step to redo.

- **The gate is Porter's, with Keller inside it.** No position ships without named trade-offs in three lists — customers not served, offers not made, deals walked away from — each read for real cost, and activity fit shown, which is also the position's defense: copying the sentence is free, copying the activity system and the trade-offs is not. Inside the gate, Keller's test sorts every element into points of parity (delivered, recorded, never sold as the distinction) and points of difference (wanted by buyers, delivered today, actually distinguishing against the frame). Neumeier's onlyness statement appears as a forcing exercise only, its answer sent back through the tests. Moon's convergence warning is posted at the gate in one line: rivals who benchmark each other converge.

- **The framing axiom is labeled as framing.** Ries and Trout's founding insight — a position lives in the prospect's mind; focus beats breadth — frames the work, supported better by the later memory research than by the original books. Their "laws" carry no procedural weight: the leadership claim in particular failed against the historical data (pioneers fail about half the time; eventual leaders enter years later on average), and launch order appears nowhere in the procedure as a rule.

- **The tensions are resolved in the open.** Differentiation versus availability: scoped by buying type. Category: chosen last, created rarely. Trade-offs versus value innovation: the build sides with trade-offs, because that claim can be audited in a firm's own activities and the other side's dataset was never shown. Focus versus reach: narrow in position and first segment, broad in memory across entry points. Each resolution is stated in the skill where the procedure depends on it, not only here.

- **Chain and boundaries are encoded.** The output is a positioning record whose first block — owned concept, market category, competitive frame, best-fit customers, trade-offs — is formatted for direct consumption as the message square of the one-page-marketing-plan skill. Comms owns how the position is said; positioning does not write launch copy. Fundraising owns one concept inside a raise and borrows the finished position; positioning is set for a market, not for a pitch, and investor pressure to change it is treated as evidence for a re-run, never as an edit. Sources that did not make the foundation — category-creation programs, blue-ocean value innovation, a company-DNA typology — are named once in Limits as roads not taken, with the reasons.

- **Publish shape from the first draft.** Output contract with the plain-style writing rule and loose-bullet formatting; a composite worked example in `references/` (invented names throughout — a job-costing tool for electrical contractors repositioned out of a borrowed accounting frame); limits and boundaries in the skill itself, including the uneven evidence base stated plainly.

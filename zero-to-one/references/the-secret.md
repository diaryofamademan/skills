# The secret

Step 1 of the procedure, worked slowly. The secret is the load-bearing claim of the whole run: it is what the venture knows that the market does not, and every later step — the monopoly read, the beachhead, the power law — is priced off it.

## The contrarian question

Thiel's interview question, and the run's starting gun: **what important truth do very few people agree with you on?**

The question is a trap for consensus answers. A truth everyone agrees with ("social media matters," "AI is big") cannot be a secret, however true it is — the advantage in it has already been competed away by the agreeing. A contrarian answer that is merely contrary (a hot take, a provocation) fails the other side of the test: it must be a truth, with evidence, not a posture.

The answer takes one sentence. If it cannot be said in one sentence, it is not yet a secret; it is a research program, and the run records it as such.

## The two kinds

**Secrets about nature.** Facts about the world that are true and not yet widely known: a cost that has quietly fallen by an order of magnitude, a behavior a device now makes measurable, a material or method that works outside the lab for the first time. Nature secrets are usually found by looking where others are not looking — unfashionable fields, unfashionable combinations, the edges of a discipline. They expire as they diffuse; the run asks how long this one stays secret.

**Secrets about people.** Truths about what people actually want or will actually do, which are hidden not by difficulty but by convention: things people know and do not say, or do not know because the respectable account of their behavior forbids seeing it. People secrets are often protected by taboo or by professional embarrassment, which is why they last longer than nature secrets — and why stating them in a record takes care. State the claim, not a verdict on any person.

## The three tests

1. **The diffusion test.** If everyone believed this tomorrow, would the venture's advantage survive? If the edge is the truth itself (a better technology, a real behavior), diffusion erodes it slowly and the durability step takes the question. If the edge is only that others do not know, the advantage is an arbitrage with an expiry date, and the record names the date it can foresee.

2. **The evidence test.** What says this is true, beyond the conviction of the person who holds it? A secret held on conviction alone is recorded as a belief. Beliefs can be right; the record simply refuses to let them borrow the standing of findings.

3. **The disprover test.** What observation would prove the secret false? A secret with no statable disprover cannot be tested, only preached. The run records the disprover beside the secret, and the change triggers in the record's close include it: if the disproving observation arrives, the run is re-done.

## Failure shapes

- **The consensus in contrarian dress.** "Nobody believes in X" said of a thing the speaker's whole industry believes in. Check the agreement claim against who actually disagrees, by name or by behavior.

- **The secret that is a feature.** A product detail competitors have not shipped yet is a lead, not a secret. Secrets are about the world or about people; features are about the roadmap, and roadmaps leak on schedule.

- **The secret too big to use.** A true claim about everything ("institutions are decaying") that implies no specific venture move. A usable secret bites somewhere specific: it picks the beachhead, prices the offer, or kills a rival's plan. If it implies nothing, it decorates the record and decides nothing.

- **The secret that must stay secret to work, published in the record.** Some secrets lose value when stated to an audience. The output contract's answer is the family's standing rule — one record, shown whole — applied with judgment about audience: the record shown to a team is whole; the record's circulation list is the user's decision, and the run notes any secret whose value depends on narrow circulation.

# Worked example — the set that failed, and the three questions that replaced it

This is the run the method was distilled from, told in de-identified form: shapes only, no names, no companies, no private figures. The subject is a founder-operator who works with an AI agent daily and answers by voice note.

## The failure

The interviewer's first sets were mined from the subject's own tools: what the dashboards showed, what the week's numbers were, what a project had shipped. Every question was, in effect, "report your data back to me." The subject rejected the set wholesale, in one line: the questions were unanswerable homework. Each one required a lookup before an opinion could start, and none of them hung on anything a stranger would care about. The interviewer had done no homework and had mistaken the subject's procedure for the subject's material.

## The replacement set

Three questions, each built topic-first, receipt second, homework attached. All three were answered by voice note the same day, and the answers became three posts.

- **Starting over after a pivot.** Universal subject: starting over. Receipt: the subject had closed one business chapter and rebuilt from the audience the first one had gathered. Numbers attached: none needed; the question is pure experience, and the set said so.

- **Running a company with no employees.** Universal subject: what machines are doing to work. Receipt: the subject's company genuinely runs on one person plus agents, that week included. Numbers attached: the interviewer attached the headcount and the rough output figures from material the subject had already shared, so the answer could start from opinion, not from counting.

- **Why people pay for confidence instead of proof.** Universal subject: trust, and what people actually buy. Receipt: the subject sells a product whose buyers ask for certainty the product cannot honestly give. Numbers attached: the interviewer attached a checked public figure on what the relevant market spends on reassurance, source named.

## The rebuild

A fourth question was first framed as valuation math: what a stake might be worth under stated assumptions. The subject sent it back and named the fix himself: ask about wealth creation, not valuation. The interviewer rebuilt it, and did the homework first this time: how many millionaires a major public offering had minted that day (public, checked that morning), how much a city's housing had jumped on the news, how many millionaires a prior famous offering had created. The question went out with those numbers attached, and was answered within minutes.

## What the example proves

The same subject, the same week, the same interviewer. The mined set produced zero answers; the built set produced four. The difference was not the topics' cleverness. It was the order: topic from the world, receipt from the life, homework done by the interviewer before the subject was asked anything.

# Question patterns — the five rules as pass-and-fail pairs

Grade a set against these pairs before it ships. Every subject below is generic: a bakery owner, a retired athlete, a first-time founder, a nurse who became a manager.

## Rule 1 — Universal subject first

- **Passes:** "What did closing your first bakery teach you about starting over?" The topic is starting over; a stranger cares before the answer starts.

- **Fails:** "How is the new ordering system working at the bakery?" The topic is a tool at one workplace. It needs the job to matter, so it dies with a stranger.

## Rule 2 — The receipt rule

- **Passes:** "You turned down the biggest contract of your career last spring. What does ambition cost?" The week supplies the receipt (the turned-down contract); the topic is ambition, which came from the world.

- **Fails:** "Walk me through how you evaluated that contract." The workday is mined for a topic and returns procedure. Nobody outside the job can use the answer.

## Rule 3 — Homework first

- **Passes:** "A third of new restaurants close in year one [source named, attached]. Yours did not. What did you do in month two that the statistic says most people skip?" The interviewer found and checked the number; the subject only has to riff.

- **Fails:** "What share of restaurants close in year one, and how did your numbers compare?" The subject is being asked to report. The interviewer has the internet too; the lookup was its job.

## Rule 4 — The riff test

- **Passes:** "Thirty seconds: is a great team born or built?" Opinion, instant, answerable while walking.

- **Fails:** "How many people have you hired, fired, and promoted across your career?" A census, not a question. The subject would have to count before they could think.

## Rule 5 — Role rotation

- **Passes:** a set of three: the founder on starting over, the investor on a loss that taught more than a win, the parent on what work took from home that year. Three roles, three universal subjects, no desk in sight.

- **Fails:** a set of three: the founder on the roadmap, the founder on the hiring pipeline, the founder on the quarter. One role, three procedures. The set collapses onto the job.

## The gate, applied

A question that passes its own rule can still fail the gate. Before a set ships, each question must be able to produce, on demand: the universal subject in one line with no job words in it, the receipt as a specific story, and the numbers attached with sources (or the explicit note that none are needed). If any of the three cannot be produced, the question is rebuilt or dropped, and the set ships short.

# Sources and credits

Every source behind this skill, with what was taken from it. All material is paraphrased and adapted; no source text is reproduced beyond short, attributed formulations (the hour-lost formulation in `five-focusing-steps.md` is Goldratt's, stated as his).

| Source | What this skill takes from it |
| --- | --- |
| Eliyahu M. Goldratt and Jeff Cox, *The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement*, North River Press, 1984 | The Theory of Constraints itself: the constraint, throughput as the goal, and the five focusing steps |
| Eliyahu M. Goldratt, *Theory of Constraints*, North River Press, 1990 | The method stated as doctrine, including throughput accounting and the inertia warning in the fifth step |
| Eliyahu M. Goldratt, *Critical Chain*, North River Press, 1997 | The method applied to project chains, where the constraint is a path rather than a machine |
| Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford, *The Phoenix Project*, IT Revolution, 2013 | The software translation of *The Goal*: work made visible, the four types of work, unplanned work as a tax, and the hero constraint (Brent) |
| Gene Kim, Jez Humble, Patrick Debois, and John Willis, *The DevOps Handbook*, IT Revolution, 2016 | The practices behind the software translation: flow, feedback, and limiting work in progress |
| Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble, and Gene Kim, *Accelerate*, IT Revolution, 2018 | The delivery measurements: throughput and stability as the pair that defines a delivery system's performance |
| Donald G. Reinertsen, *The Principles of Product Development Flow*, Celeritas, 2009 | The flow mathematics: queues, batch size, work-in-progress constraints, and cost of delay |
| John D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW", *Operations Research*, 1961 | Little's Law: cycle time = work in progress ÷ throughput |
| Walter Isaacson, *Elon Musk*, Simon & Schuster, 2023 | The Algorithm (question every requirement, delete, simplify and optimize, accelerate cycle time, automate), the order the elevate step runs in; the same material the first-principles skill carries in its references |
| W. Edwards Deming, *Out of the Crisis*, MIT Press, 1986 | The background discipline: a system is managed as a whole, and variation is a property of the system, not a verdict on the people in it |

Background explainers consulted while building: "Theory of Constraints Explained" (Learnsignal, https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/theory-of-constraints-five-focusing-steps-explained/) for a plain restatement of the five steps, and summaries of Isaacson's Algorithm chapter for step wording cross-checks.

## Winning Through Intimidation — Robert J. Ringer

1. **Posture and leverage.** Counterparties treat you according to the frame you set at first contact; open as a peer with options, not a supplicant. *(credibility; all tracks)*
2. **Image, agreements, performance.** Professional presentation, documented terms, and real delivery reinforce each other; polish without substance fails. *(credibility; pitch)*
3. **Qualify before investing time.** Many “buyers” are curious; confirm principal vs intermediary, check size, stage, and decision authority. *(qualify fast)*
4. **Few logical targets.** Prioritize counterparties who fit on paper, not a long tail of hope. *(target list)*
5. **Delay kills deals.** Answer quickly, push diligence and site visits, move to signing before distraction wins. *(ABC; close)*
6. **Late interest and competition.** Serious buyers often appear when a deal is nearly done; use real competition ethically; do not drop a signed path for a rumor. *(momentum; second offer; M&A)*
7. **Closing friction.** Expect lawyer markups and late surprises; warn parties and stay cooperative. *(close; M&A)*
8. **Cash in bank.** Verbal yes and unsigned terms are not closed; track wires. *(ABC; all tracks)*
9. **Process control.** One team runs messaging; avoid uncontrolled side channels. *(M&A; startup process)*
10. **Next prospect.** Treat a pass as routine; move on quickly when prepared. *(ABC)*
11. **Reality.** Deal with facts as they are; ignore noise that does not move the process. *(qualify fast)*

## Angel — Jason Calacanis

See blog notes: [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-angel-by-jason-calacanis), [Haley Bryant](https://www.haleymbryant.com/blog/angel-by-jason-calacanis-summary).

1. **Back the founder.** Angels invest in people; lead with founder–market fit and commitment. *(pitch; startup)*
2. **Four narrative pillars.** What you build, why you, why now, unfair advantage. *(pitch)*
3. **Tactical diligence questions.** Competition, revenue model, pricing, CAC, top failure modes—short precise answers beat rambling. *(credibility)*
4. **Traction before the ask.** Product in market, growth or revenue, strong team, credible co-investors, adequate runway after close—idea-only pitches are weak. *(qualify; startup)*
5. **Valuation tied to metrics.** Explain the number with data; followers often accept a lead’s price if they like the company. *(lead/anchor)*
6. **Real vs artificial urgency.** Manufactured scarcity is discounted; follow up on a set schedule with real momentum. *(momentum; close)*
7. **Capital efficiency.** Prior spend and founder pay signal discipline. *(credibility)*
8. **Monthly investor updates.** Cash, burn, runway, wins, losses, specific asks; silence looks like failure. *(reporting; ABC)*
9. **Pro rata and information rights.** Plan cap table and side letters for angel terms. *(close; startup)*
10. **Bridges need a story.** What changed since the last round. *(startup)*
11. **Co-investor network.** Warm paths through existing investors’ syndicates. *(target list; lead)*
12. **M&A: bought, not sold.** Best exits often involve competing acquirers; acquihires often disappoint investors. *(M&A)*
13. **Power law for funds.** Most angel bets fail; a few winners drive returns; high-volume screening. *(LP fund)*

## Positioning — Al Ries & Jack Trout

Summary source: [BestWriting — Positioning summary](https://bestwriting.com/positioning-summary)

1. **Positioning is in the mind.** Start from what the VC, LP, or acquirer already believes; connect your story to that—not a raw feature list. *(pitch; all tracks)*
2. **Oversimplify.** Cut the deck to one sharp idea; investors remember almost nothing. *(pitch; headlines)*
3. **Own one concept.** One idea per name—the line a partner repeats Monday or an LP repeats in IC. *(pitch; LP strategy)*
4. **Be first in something.** First in a category tends to hold; if not in the big category, narrow until you can be first, then expand (pairs with Thiel’s small-market dominance). *(pitch; LP fund)*
5. **New category needs a reference.** Say what the idea is *not* against the old way (Uncola pattern). *(startup pitch)*
6. **Don’t attack the leader head-on.** Go around: tie to their frame or reposition their weakness; “we’re better” invites “why aren’t you winning?” *(competition; M&A narrative)*
7. **Leadership claims must match belief.** Empty superlatives and unearned “No. 1” claims backfire. *(credibility)*
8. **Avoid the everybody trap.** Sacrifice breadth; pick a narrow target you can own. *(target list; LP strategy)*
9. **Line extension blurs position.** One name, one meaning; a new strategy may need a new vehicle/name. *(LP; startup)*
10. **Platform can’t borrow unearned product credit.** Fund or company credibility flows from the core strategy’s position. *(credibility; LP)*
11. **Consistency across rounds and funds.** Repeat the same core position for years; change tactics, not the anchor story; re-ups depend on a stable identity. *(pitch; LP re-ups)*
12. **Concentrate resources.** Saturate one investor or LP segment, win, then roll out; time publicity to the position. *(target list; momentum)*

## Breakthrough Advertising — Eugene M. Schwartz

Summary source: [Valchanova — Breakthrough Advertising notes](https://valchanova.me/breakthrough-advertising-copywriting-book-review/)

1. **Channel existing desire.** Copy cannot create appetite—investors, LPs, and acquirers already want something; aim your pitch at that want after research. *(pitch; cold outreach)*
2. **Lead with the strongest shared desire.** Urgency, persistence, and breadth across targets pick the headline desire; proof follows. *(headlines; outreach)*
3. **Outcomes before features.** Subject lines and slide titles = desire; specs justify below. *(deck; updates)*
4. **Match message to awareness.** Ready investor → terms + reason to act now; warm but unconvinced → new proof/mechanism; cold → name problem/desire first, then your answer. *(cold outreach; ABC; close)*
5. **Match market sophistication.** Fresh category → direct claim; crowded → new mechanism; jaded (e.g. another seed fund) → identification (who you serve). *(pitch; LP positioning)*
6. **Headline job = next line read.** First line of email, cold note, and slide title earn attention; body reframes reality. *(outreach; deck)*
7. **Intensify with specifics.** Proof in action, time-stretched benefits, endorsements, contrast, cost of inaction, summary. *(pitch; investor updates)*
8. **Belief in steps.** Stack small agreed facts from what they already accept toward valuation or thesis. *(pitch; negotiation)*
9. **Reframe objections.** Too complex → simplify; too niche → escalate importance; too expensive → compare to costlier alternative. *(negotiation; M&A; startup)*
10. **Contrast alternatives.** Where incumbents fail the investor; before/after or side-by-side. *(competition; LP fund)*
11. **Editorial tone and frank limits.** Sincere updates that state weaknesses and who the deal is *not* for make positives believable. *(updates; credibility)*
12. **Length fits the job.** Warm + ready → short; cold LP or acquirer → enough to build desire and belief; boring fails, not long. *(outreach; LP; M&A)*

## Pitch Anything — Oren Klaff

Summaries: [Deven Davis](https://www.devendavis.com/book-summaries/pitch-anything); [The Rabbit Hole (blas.com)](https://blas.com/pitch-anything/). Additional search excerpts: [Neuromarketing review](https://www.neurosciencemarketing.com/blog/articles/pitch-anything-by-oren-klaff.htm) (search excerpt only).

1. **Croc brain filter.** Safety, novelty, simplicity first—investors reject cognitive load. *(pitch)*
2. **Frame control.** Stronger frame absorbs weaker; explaining your authority means you lost frame. *(credibility; negotiation)*
3. **Counter frames.** Power → light defiance; time → tighter constraint; analyst → story pull-back. *(negotiation)*
4. **Local status.** Avoid beta traps (supplicant rituals); lead with domain expertise. *(credibility)*
5. **~20-minute structure:** you + big idea → budget/secret sauce → offer terms → stack frames. *(pitch)*
6. **Intrigue.** Short story with tension holds attention. *(pitch)*
7. **Prize frame.** You qualify investors/LPs; they are not doing you a favor. *(lead/anchor; momentum)*
8. **Time frame.** Real deadline and allocation limit—only if true (pairs with Kim/scarcity). *(close)*
9. **Stack frames.** Intrigue + prize + time + moral/mission → hot cognition. *(pitch)*
10. **Hot before cold.** Emotion decides; data justifies (pairs with Kim). *(pitch)*
11. **Neediness kills.** Validation-seeking drops status; full pipeline enables walk-away. *(qualify; ABC)*
12. **STRONG outline:** Set frame, Tell story, Reveal intrigue, Offer prize, Nail hook, Get deal. *(pitch structure)*

## Zero to One — Peter Thiel (with Blake Masters)

Blog summary: [Medium commentary summary](https://xdev200200.medium.com/zero-to-one-commentary-summary-3695704017f9) (plus maintainer paraphrase from published material).

1. **Contrarian truth.** Strong narratives rest on a valuable insight few share yet. *(pitch)*
2. **Monopoly vs competition.** Lasting value escapes pure competition; define your market honestly—neither inflate TAM nor hide dominance. *(pitch; credibility)*
3. **Start small, then expand.** Dominate a narrow market before adjacencies. *(startup pitch)*
4. **Durability.** Defensibility over years: technology edge, network effects, scale, brand. *(pitch; M&A valuation story)*
5. **Seven investor screens.** Engineering, timing, monopoly potential, team, distribution, durability, secret—address in deck. *(pitch)*
6. **Power law returns.** A single company can return a fund; show outcome scale VCs need. *(startup; LP)*
7. **Concentrated fund strategy.** Few bets with multibillion potential vs diversification theater. *(LP fund)*
8. **Definite plan.** Specific roadmap beats vague iteration-only stories. *(credibility)*
9. **Sell from strength.** Weak sellers sell because vision ran out; buyers misprice; show roadmap value in M&A. *(M&A)*
10. **Engineered momentum.** Apparent frenzy usually follows deliberate outreach and narrative work. *(momentum; ABC)*
11. **Distribution matters.** Channel and unit economics are as important as product. *(pitch)*
12. **Team signals.** Cofounder history, lean board, equity alignment over high cash pay. *(credibility; startup)*

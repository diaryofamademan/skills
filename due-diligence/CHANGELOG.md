# Changelog

## 0.2.10 — 2026-11-19

- **Step 5's writing rule is now a called-in pass, not a reminder.** The "write it like a person" paragraph asked for plain style in prose, the same shape that failed in the sibling profiling skill the same week: its field dossiers shipped in analyst register and the reader rejected them as legalese. Step 5 now names the procedure: run the work-humanizer skill in rewrite mode over the summary and the sheet's prose (statuses, quotes, names, and dates never change), then apply the writing-styles skill's plain rules. The summary is the reader version and the deliverable; the full sheet is the record under it. A gate closes the step: the sheet is not done until the pass has run on the exact text being delivered. The two standing habits (explain registry terms in plain words on first use; loose bulleted lists) ride inside the gate. Field origin: the owner's correction of 2026-11-19 — the writing skills exist to be called in by the skills that need them. Profiling carries the matching change in its v0.3.15.

## 0.2.9 — 2026-11-18

Three steps absorbed from a field comparison against an outside OSINT diligence workflow, each folded into the module it belongs to rather than added as a separate system. The comparison found the outside workflow's sourcing comparable in breadth; these were the three steps this skill lacked.

- **Procurement records are now revenue evidence.** The entity module gains a step: published contract awards, tender results, and vendor registrations are primary-source evidence of real revenue and a real customer, because the buyer keeps the record. A claimed trading history is checked against buyers' records first. The source map grades procurement records primary, ranked with the registry and filing evidence, and states the limits: private buyers publish nothing, framework values are ceilings not payments, and a vendor registration proves eligibility, not a sale.

- **OFAC's own search is a named step in the screen.** Step 2 now runs the US sanctions lists through OFAC's own search tool (sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov) directly, as a step of its own, alongside the aggregator pass — an aggregator can lag or mis-map a list update, and the direct search is cheap. The screening log names both passes. The source map names the tool as the direct route to the SDN and consolidated lists.

- **New module: the digital-estate timeline check.** Step 3 gains a step that reads the start of the subject's online trail — domain registration dates and history, the earliest archived copies of their websites, the creation dates of long-standing public accounts — and sets it against the claimed founding and operating timeline. A claimed history that starts years before the earliest trace grades as a claim-versus-record mismatch, with dates quoted. Shared infrastructure with unrelated brands is a lead toward a common operator. The cautions are in the step: an aged domain can be bought, so the trace must show the claimed activity, not just an old name; and a thin early web grades unverified, not contradicted, unless the claim itself is about an online presence. The source map grades the step's records (primary for dates, mirror for archived content), and the fact-sheet format lists the module.

## 0.2.8 — 2026-11-18

- **Reference checks split into their own skill.** The module and its question bank leave this package. Reference checks now run the standalone `reference-checks` skill: witness verification (confirming a reference is who they claim to be before their answers count), the question bank, the channel forms, and answer grading live there, as the single source of truth — nothing of the bank is copied here. This skill keeps a short call in Step 3: what reference checks are for, that they run the `reference-checks` skill, and the one rule that governs every contact this skill can set in motion — each contact approved by the user in advance, one at a time, with no standing approval. Guardrail 2 now names the `reference-checks` skill as the second exception to no-contact. `references/reference-check-questions.md` is deleted, and the references index and README point to the sibling skill instead. Public complaint records stay here, swept read-only under adverse media — they were never contact.

- **The four-skill pass, run over the whole package.** The standing rule is that every build runs the four skills; the earlier builds shipped without that pass, and this entry records what it changed and what it checked and held.

  - *First principles.* Step 0 promised "all modules" at Standard and Deep, but several modules run only when the commitment triggers them — the depth lines now say so, and the module set reads as what it is: a menu keyed to the commitment's shape, not a checklist. The paper module's pay question is widened from their token to their own paper in any form, with token pay routed to the token-offerings module — the overlap between the two is closed. Adverse media now states its own limit in the module text: a media item grades nothing until a record module closes it. Every other module was tested against the same question — could its answers change a decision or a protection? — and held.

  - *Game theory.* Step 3 gains a sequencing rule: records before contact. The record sweep is invisible to the subject; a verification call or a reference contact is a move the subject can learn about, and a subject who learns early can coach a reference, revise a claim, or tidy what can still be tidied. Contact now runs last, after the record is settled. The claim-pinning rule below is the same point from the other side. The paper module's structure questions (staged delivery, escrow, security) were checked as the mechanism-design answers they are, and held.

  - *Comms.* The fact-sheet summary is now bound by the claim sort: it restates graded findings only, every sentence traceable to an item below it, because the checker's summary is a voice, not a witness — the record is the witness. No new claim enters at summary level. The skill's own prose was read against the plain-writing gate and held, apart from the edits in this entry.

  - *Fundraising.* Claim verification gains intake discipline: every material claim is pinned in checkable form at intake and assigned to the module or primary source that tests it, and a claim no source can test is logged then, as untestable — not discovered as a surprise gap at the end. The verified-only discipline elsewhere (market data labeled signal and dated, the re-run before signing when a sweep has aged) was checked and held.

- **Generalization pass.** The package was re-read as a stranger would read it, and written for any user. The Step 3 vault note named one user's credential system and project; it now reads "the user's own credential vault," and the same fix was applied to the 0.2.3 entry below — the only other place the user's own systems were named. The crypto modules (on-chain record, token offerings, the token distress signal) are framed as what they are: domain modules that run when the commitment supplies wallets or a token, not the skill's default frame. A grep of the package for the original user's name, companies, and credential tools returns nothing.

## 0.2.7 — 2026-11-18

- **The reference-check question bank.** The reference-checks module shipped in 0.2.6 with its rules — approval one at a time, open contact, logging — but no scripts; this build gives it its questions, in a new reference file, `references/reference-check-questions.md`. The bank was built through a four-skill review pass — first principles for the question architecture, game theory for what each answer costs the respondent, comms for the channel forms, fundraising for the closer — and the architecture is the pass's output: three question types in fixed order (**Verify**, a fact checkable against a record; **Event**, a specific past instance, so honesty is asked as an event and never as a character grade; **Signal**, a closer that costs the respondent something to answer). The sets are split by audience — a former boss (authority, bad-news speed, the re-check question, money disputes), a peer (reliance and the team-again closer), and a customer or counterparty (delivery, bad-news speed, disputes in either direction, claim tests built from what the subject told us). Questions to a volunteered reference are built from the subject's own claims, quoted back; back-channel references get the standard sets. The opening offers attribution tiers (on the record, background, not by name) up front, and every question is written as if the subject will hear it repeated. Channel forms: phone runs the full set in order with the closer last; email runs five questions with the closer second, so a partial reply still returns the highest-value answers; LinkedIn carries the opening and one Verify question only. The grading notes score the closer's gradient — instant yes, qualified yes, pause, deflection, refusal — and grade a declined closer as an answer, not a gap. Every set ends with "Who else should I talk to?", each name logged for the user's approval — the chain is how the back-channel list grows.

## 0.2.6 — 2026-11-18

Earned by a retrospective test: v0.2.5 was run, on paper, against five closed commitments that had gone bad, and scored — one it would have blocked, three partial, one it would have missed entirely. The pattern across the misses was consistent: the skill verified the other side and never protected this side — the contract, the keys, the token. Five of the additions below close that side; the international sweep and reference checks came out of the same review.

- **New module: the paper — the contract itself.** Required at Standard and above whenever money or assets move under a contract. Five questions answered from the signed or draft text: forum and governing law (a missing dispute clause is a finding, not a footnote); whether our claim is secured (a filed security interest, collateral, a guarantee — or the sheet says "unsecured creditor" in its own words); delivery staged, escrowed, or milestone-based against all-up-front; records and audit rights with a deadline and a consequence; and how much of the pay is in their token, priced at the zero case. An unsecured all-up-front structure grades as a finding, not a footnote.

- **Depth now follows access, not size.** Step 0 gains the rule: when a commitment grants keys, systems, signing authority, or custody of funds, the depth is set by that access — a short trial with production keys is a Deep check. Person checks that granted access now carry a closing exit item: access revoked, shared keys rotated, answered when the engagement ends or reported open.

- **The on-chain module reads both sides of the wire.** Before access is granted or funds move, the control read now also runs on the user's own contracts the commitment touches (single admin key or multisig, threshold, timelock) and on the destination wallets that will hold the assets (owner, signing threshold, named people or anonymous keys). Custody concentrated in one staff wallet is a finding readable before the transfer.

- **New module: token offerings (Deep).** For commitments that buy a token or take one as pay: the offering checked on EDGAR (registration, a Form D, or a stated exemption the facts support — none of the three is an unregistered sale and a finding); the token contract's control read (mint, pause, treasury keys); insider allocations and the unlock schedule in writing; and a dated shipped-vs-promised comparison.

- **The distress module reads the market for crypto counterparties.** A project's own token joins the distress signals: price against launch price, liquidity, listings gained and lost. A collapsed or delisted house token is a distress finding even when court and lien records are clean — labeled market data, graded as a signal. The source map grades token market data accordingly.

- **New module: the international sweep.** For non-US subjects: home registry first, OpenCorporates as the map, the GLEIF LEI record for identity and parents; the free primary registries named (UK Companies House, France, Germany, Canada federal, Australia ABN/ASIC); ICIJ Offshore Leaks for offshore structures; the global lists (OFAC, EU, UK, UN, World Bank debarred) with OpenSanctions as aggregator; courts through BAILII, CanLII, AustLII, and WorldLII. The limits are stated in the module: pay-per-document registries (Singapore, Hong Kong, India), agent-only registries (Cayman), EU privacy thinning people records, transliteration as part of the variant set, and verification outside the US routing to email, LinkedIn, or the registered agent because the call route reaches US numbers.

- **New module: reference checks (optional, Deep).** Two classes, weighted differently: volunteered references (curated — used to test consistency against the record) and back-channel references found independently (former bosses and peers for a person; customers and counterparties for an entity), with public complaint records (BBB, regulator complaint summaries) swept read-only. Contact by phone, email, or LinkedIn under the verification call's discipline, extended: open, no pretext, every contact approved by the user in advance, one at a time — no standing approval. Each contact is logged (who, how, what was asked, what was said); a refusal or a cannot-reach is recorded as itself. Guardrail 2 now names exactly two exceptions to no-contact: verification calls and reference checks. The fact-sheet format's module list is updated to match.

## 0.2.5 — 2026-11-17

- **The ownership graph is worked whole, and principals are checked in proportion to their control.** Step 3 now states the trace rule: ownership is followed up to natural persons (the ultimate beneficial owners) or to a listed/regulated endpoint, and down through controlled subsidiaries, with every layer named, screened, and read in its registry — and an unpierceable layer reported as a finding, not a footnote. ## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-17

The core mechanics upgrade, from a deep review of the owner's due-diligence source list against v0.1.x (twenty gaps assessed; the six core items built here; the reverse-direction commitment, the supplied-materials branch, flag-to-protection mapping, and the corroboration test are recorded as the second wave). The procedure is now six steps: screening stands as its own step between identity and the sweep, so grading moves to Step 4 and the fact sheet to Step 5.

- **Step 1 gains resolution mechanics.** A variant set (misspellings, former names, transliterations, phonetic equivalents), selector pivots (each anchor is a door into the others), a candidate set carried with confirm/eliminate criteria until it resolves or excludes, widen/narrow discipline with a stopping rule, and brand-to-entity resolution routes (statutory disclosure, trademark proprietor, invoice numbers, the chain walked from any confirmed member).
- **The entity module is rebuilt on registry mechanics.** Company-number search, the filing-history timeline read in order, former names collected as search keys, the charges and secured-party registers read for hidden counterparties, officer and address pivots, LEI/GLEIF parent data, OpenCorporates graded as index-not-source, ICIJ and OCCRP as lead-grade, and the four failure modes of ownership tracing — with the documented opacity point itself reported as a finding.
- **Screening becomes Step 2, with a log.** Subject, entities, parents and subsidiaries, and every named principal, against sanctions, PEP (named explicitly), and debarment lists; hits confirmed at the primary list; the lists, terms, and date recorded in the fact sheet so a negative is evidence.
- **New module: vendor cyber and data protection.** Exposure tiering first; certification claims verified at the issuer's register; breach history from notification registers and regulator actions; public posture (email authentication, TLS, security.txt); document requests (SOC 2 report, DPA, subprocessor list) routed through the user as legitimately held material; a refusal reported as a finding.
- **New module: on-chain record, bounded.** Only addresses and contracts the commitment supplies: explorer history and sanctions screening of the addresses; contract control read from chain state at a noted block (owner, proxy admin, upgrade and pause authority, multisig threshold, timelock, vault curator, caps, oracles); clustering labeled inference, never fact; a counterparty that stays pseudonymous reported as an unresolved identity.
- **New reference: the shell-company indicator catalogue.** Weighted indicators across registration, officers, operations, financial, and structural categories, scored as a cluster ("any one is common and innocent; four together is the answer"), with both counter-errors named — opacity is not wrongdoing, and substance is not legitimacy.
- Guardrail conflicts found in the reviewed sources — leaked-credential corpora, sockpuppet personas, dark-web monitoring, family-member pivots — were not imported; the guardrails are unchanged.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-17

First build, in publish shape. The skill was split out of psychological-profiling (whose v0.3.9 added a bounded legal-record sweep to its gather) on a simple finding: verification and inference are different disciplines, and housing them together weakens both. Profiling keeps a light sweep for conversation prep; this skill is the full check, run before a commitment.

- **Procedure:** name the commitment and set the depth (light, standard, deep); resolve identity; sweep six modules (entity and corporate record, legal record, regulatory and licensing, financial-distress signals, claim verification, adverse media); grade the findings; write the fact sheet and hand off.
- **Identity before findings:** a record counts as the subject's only on two independent identifiers; namesakes are logged with the fact that separates them; an unresolvable identity is reported as a finding, not papered over.
- **Statuses, not scores:** every item returns verified, partially verified, unverified, contradicted, or not found in the sources swept — with source and date. Red flags are graded for the specific commitment: blocks it, must be explained, note and proceed.
- **Outcomes over allegations** throughout the legal and media modules; patterns graded above instances; base rates stated so an ordinary operating life is not flagged as scandal.
- **Boundaries:** public or legitimately held material only; no pretexting and no contact as investigation; professional scope by default; the fact sheet is not a consumer report, and the Fair Credit Reporting Act boundary is named in the guardrails; credit reports and personal debt data are stated not to exist as public records.
- **The feed:** the fact sheet's handoff block formats verified facts for psychological-profiling to cite as its record tier. This skill never infers personality; profiling never has to verify identity.

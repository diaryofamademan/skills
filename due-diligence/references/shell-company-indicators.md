# Shell-company indicators

Support for the entity module in Step 3: the signs that a company is a shell — an entity with a legal existence and little or no life behind it. No single indicator means anything. Real companies trip one or two by accident of age, size, or jurisdiction. The method is the cluster: **any one indicator is common and innocent; four or more together, across different categories, is the answer** — and the fact sheet reports the cluster and its count, not a vibe.

## The catalogue

**Registration signs** (weight: low alone, they are cheap to produce)
- Formed very recently relative to the history claimed for it.
- Formed in a secrecy-friendly jurisdiction with no other visible tie to that place.
- Registered agent is a formation service; the registered address is a mailbox, a virtual office, or an address shared by hundreds of entities.
- No filings after formation — no annual reports, no amendments — or filings made only when a transaction required them.

**Officer signs** (weight: medium)
- Officers or directors who appear in the same roles across many unrelated entities (the nominee pattern).
- A single officer who is simultaneously director, secretary, and treasurer, with no other named human anywhere in the record.
- Officers whose other traces — licenses, press, professional profiles — do not exist or do not match the claimed business.

**Operations signs** (weight: medium to high — a living business leaves operational residue)
- No employees, premises, or operating footprint findable anywhere: no hiring, no reviews, no customers, no suppliers naming it.
- A website that is a brochure with no named people, no address that resolves, and a domain registered far more recently than the claimed founding.
- No tax, license, or permit footprint in the place it claims to operate.

**Financial signs** (weight: high where visible)
- No charges, banking, or credit footprint at all for a company claiming significant trade — real trade leaves secured-party and litigation traces.
- Accounts (where filing regimes require them) that are dormant, micro-entity, or nil-activity filings under a story of large operations.
- Capital and share structure that obscures: bearer-like arrangements, circular holdings, or layers whose only visible function is distance.

**Structural signs** (weight: high)
- Ownership that stops at a nominee, a trust, or a threshold — held just below a disclosure line (24.9% is a number chosen, not a coincidence).
- Layers across jurisdictions with no operational reason any layer can state.
- A chain that re-forms: the same people, address, or business under a new entity shortly after the old one acquires obligations, judgments, or press.

## The two counter-errors

**Opacity is not wrongdoing.** Privacy is a legitimate reason for structure — safety, estate planning, competitive discretion — and plenty of real businesses are hard to see into. The indicator cluster grades the *risk to this commitment* (you cannot verify who you are dealing with), not the morality of the structure. Where tracing stops, the fact sheet reports the documented stopping point (SKILL.md, Step 3), and the commitment decides what unverifiable means for it.

**Substance is not legitimacy.** A shell can be dressed: real office, real employees, real filings, real trade — maintained as a front. The cluster method cuts both ways; a company with no indicators can still be a fraud whose costs are simply being paid. The indicators screen for emptiness. They do not certify fullness, and a clean run of this catalogue is a "not found," never a clearance.

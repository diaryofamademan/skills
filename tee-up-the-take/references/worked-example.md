# Worked example — a founder launches a methods collection

A founder who rebuilt a services firm to run on AI agents is filming the video that launches the collection of methods the firm now runs on. The methods are free. The video's job: get viewers to take one method and join the list that receives new ones. Target length: about half an hour. Names, figures, and dates are illustrative; the shapes are the point.

## Step 2 output — the mined record (last 28 posts)

Winning shapes, from the counts:

- **The personal story with a hard turn.** The post about shutting the old firm down — the whole staff, down to one — drew about double the engagement of anything else in the window, and the most replies.
- **The repeatable line.** "I run my whole company with AI agents. No employees." drew strong replies — people arguing, asking, quoting it back.
- **The money story with its numbers in it.** A post telling what he raised and what the fund held took the most saves of the window — saves being the closest signal to buying intent.

Losing shape:

- **The formal announcement.** A capitalised, résumé-style launch post drew the least engagement of the window. The same story, told casually the day before, drew six times more.

## Step 3 output — the claim sort (extract)

| Claim | Sort | Witness |
|---|---|---|
| Built the firm from zero to eight figures in lifetime revenue | Needs a witness | His own published posts and the firm's public history |
| Raised about eight million for the firm, ran a fund of nearly twenty million | Needs a witness | His published fundraising post; fund announcements |
| Went from a full staff to one person, on purpose | Said by the performer | — (his decision, his story) |
| The methods will build a billion-dollar company | Goal form only | Quest beat: dated, with milestones and a public scoreboard |
| "Best methods collection in the field" | Cut | No witness exists; superlatives are confidence, not evidence |

## The riff sheet (extract — beats 1, 3, 5, 7 of 9)

**Beat 1 — The turn**
- **Question:** "Take me back to the day you decided to shut it down. What had the company become?"
- **Hit:** The staff count at the peak; the day it went to one; what it cost and what forced it.
- **Land:** "I shut down my own company — fifty people down to one — and rebuilt it with AI agents."
- **Flag:** Cold-open candidate.

**Beat 3 — The receipts**
- **Bridge:** "And none of this came from nowhere. Let me give you the receipts."
- **Question:** "What did the old company actually do, in numbers — and which of those numbers ended up teaching you a method?"
- **Hit:** Eight figures lifetime revenue; the raise; the fund; one lesson per number, each tied to the method it produced.
- **Land:** "Every method in this collection was paid for once already — in money I raised, spent, or lost."
- **Flag:** Cold-open candidate.

**Beat 5 — The quest**
- **Bridge:** "So if the billion isn't the name, what is it? It's the goal."
- **Question:** "So what is this collection actually for? What are you trying to prove with it, in public?"
- **Hit:** The billion-dollar goal in goal form; the date; the milestones; where the scoreboard lives.
- **Land:** "The goal is a billion-dollar company run by agents. The playbook is how. The numbers go up in public, win or lose."
- **Flag:** Cold-open candidate.

**Beat 7 — The callback close**
- **Bridge:** "So here's where you get it. And one last thing about those fifty people."
- **Question:** "Last thing. The fifty people — what would you tell them now?"
- **Hit:** The turn, returned to; what the one-person version does that the fifty-person version could not.
- **Land:** "Fifty people could not do what the playbook does now. One person can — because the playbook is the team."
- **Flag:** Callback to beat 1.

The remaining beats in the full sheet: proof of life (a working day with the agents), the method (one story per method, four beats' worth), and the invitation (the free collection, then the doorway: two companies a quarter, an audit first, application only).

## What the example shows

The sheet opens with the performer's best counted shape, not with the announcement. The ambition appears once, in goal form, in the quest beat — it is never the collection's name and never a result. And the announcement itself is demoted to beat 8's doorway: by the time the viewer hears what is being launched, they have heard the story, the receipts, and the quest it serves.

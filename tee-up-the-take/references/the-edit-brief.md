# The edit brief

Attach this brief to every riff sheet. It is the instruction the edit runs under when the footage arrives. The craft inside each step belongs to the production skills named at the end; the brief fixes the order and the standards.

## The five steps, in order

1. **Transcribe the full take.** One transcript, timecoded, before anything is cut. Every later decision cites the transcript, not memory of the footage. If the performer recorded a separate screen share or a separate audio track, sync all sources to the transcript first.

2. **Cut the fillers.** Remove filler words, false starts, repeated openings, and dead air, against the transcript. Cut for sense, not for silence alone: a pause that lands a line stays; a pause that searches for one goes.

3. **Build the cold open.** From the beats the sheet flagged, assemble the first ten to thirty seconds out of the strongest landable lines, cut from wherever they occur in the take. The cold open promises the video the viewer is about to get: the turn, one receipt, one repeatable line. No title card, no greeting, no throat-clearing before it.

4. **Weave for retention.** Order the remaining beats for attention, not chronology. A beat filmed last may belong second; a callback may pull its pair earlier. The test at every join is the viewer's next ten seconds, not the performer's speaking order. Restore marked callbacks at their new positions so each one still points at its pair.

5. **Verify against the sheet.** Check the finished cut against the riff sheet: every beat's Land line is present; no claim appears in the cut that the claim sort did not pass; every figure is the spoken form the sheet carried; the cold open contains only lines that also appear in the body, so the promise is one the video keeps.

## Standards

- **One take stays one take.** The weave reorders a single performance; it does not manufacture sentences out of fragments. If a join would put words in the performer's mouth they did not say in that order as one thought, the join is not made.
- **Captions from the verified transcript**, corrected against the audio, never from auto-captions alone.
- **Loudness to the platform's norm**, speech forward; music, where used at all, under the voice and never competing with it.

## Production skills the edit runs under

Transcription: the transcribe-recordings skill. Cutting and assembly: the FFmpeg skill set. Direction, pacing, and motion: the AI director and motion-graphics skills, and the channel-operator production stages where a channel system applies. The brief names them; their own rules govern inside the edit.

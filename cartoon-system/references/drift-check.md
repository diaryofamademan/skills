# The drift check — per panel, pass or fail

Every panel is checked against this list before it is approved. Each item is pass or fail. One fail fails the panel.

The panel is checked against two things: the character bible, and — through the continuity block — the approved panels before it in the same piece.

## Character identity

- **Face and build** — the face shape, the snout or muzzle, the ears, the body proportions match the character's bible entry. Pass only if the character is recognisable as the same individual at a glance, in a thumbnail, without the caption.
- **Palette** — every flat colour on the character matches the entry: coat, chest, muzzle, wardrobe, props. A near colour is a wrong colour.
- **Signature props and wardrobe** — the character's recorded props and clothes are present where the entry says they belong, drawn the same way.

## The eyes

The eyes get their own block because they drift first and carry identity most.

- **Shape and size** — the eyes match the bible's eye spec exactly: same shape, same size against the face.
- **Colour and highlights** — same colour, and highlights only if the spec has them. A plain dot eye that comes back glossy, with white highlights and a pupil, has failed — it is the most common drift in practice.
- **The brow line** — if the spec carries a brow line, it is present and drawn the same way. Expression lives in the brow and the mouth in a deadpan register; a new eye style smuggles in a new character.

## The cast rule

- Every being in frame is allowed by the bible's cast rule.
- No character appears who has no bible entry.
- Counterparties are cast as the rule requires — in the reference system, the other side is always an animal, and humans never appear.

## Continuity — against the prior approved panels

Checked against the immediately prior approved panel, and against any earlier panel the new one shares a setting, a prop, or a secondary character with.

- **Setting continuity** — a returning setting matches its earlier drawing: same elements, same arrangement, same light.
- **Prop continuity** — shared props are the same props: the same desk, the same book, the same board, drawn the same way and the same size in the paw or on the table.
- **Scale and placement across the cut** — characters hold their bible ratios from panel to panel. Nobody grows, shrinks, or swaps sides of the frame without the story asking for it.
- **Light and palette** — the piece's light and background tones hold across the cut. A panel that arrives in a different season of colour fails here even if every character is right.

## Box and padding

- After the mechanical mount, the border weight and the padding equal the frame spec on every side, and the page size matches the set.
- The drawn border inside the art is clean and complete, so the crop to it is exact. A broken or doubled drawn border fails here — the mount cannot fix a frame the model mangled.

## Line style

- The ink weight and economy match the series register: same confidence, same level of detail.
- No shift of register — glossier, busier, more rendered, or more sketch-like than the set — in either direction.

## Background

- Sparse, in palette, and consistent with the panel's earlier settings.
- No new element contradicts an earlier panel — a window where a wall was, a tree in a room.
- No writing-like marks: no letters, numbers, logos, or signage shapes anywhere in the background.

## Invented text

- No letters, numbers, words, logos, or signage anywhere in the art — on objects, on paper, on clothing, anywhere. Any instance fails the panel outright, with no exceptions. Text belongs in the text layers, set as type.

## On a fail

- **Regenerate.** The panel is generated again with its full reference set — standing anchors plus the working chain. A drifted panel is never repaired in an editor and never shipped with an apology.
- **Second failure on the same panel:** strengthen the prompt's style block. Name the failing trait explicitly — "eyes are small plain black dots, no highlights" — and generate again. The standard never drops to meet the model.
- **A late change reopens the chain.** If an early panel is regenerated after later panels exist, the later panels are re-checked against the new version, continuity block first. Any that break are regenerated. The chain only points backward at approved, current images.
- **Record the scar.** When a panel fails and the fix teaches something — a trait that drifts, a wording that holds it — the lesson goes into the character's drift notes in the bible at the end of the run.
